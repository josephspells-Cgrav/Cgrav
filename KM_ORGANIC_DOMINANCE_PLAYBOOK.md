## The 9 thesis pillars (cited)

### Pillar 1 — Proximity ceiling of the map pack
## T1 — The Proximity Ceiling of the Map Pack

**Bottom line:** The map pack is a proximity-capped surface. Google ranks it from a business's verified physical address, not its service area ([support.google.com](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091); [sterlingsky.ca](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/does-the-service-area-in-google-my-business-impact-ranking/)). One office equals one ranking anchor. Pack visibility decays with distance from that anchor, sharply in the first mile, and collapses fast in dense metros ([rankings.io](https://rankings.io/data-studies/proximity-data-study/)). Organic ranking is materially less proximity-bound — it can rank a city you serve but are not located in ([datapins.com](https://www.datapins.com/seo-for-multiple-cities/); [sterlingsky.ca](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/2025-update-how-proximity-impacts-google-organic-results/)). That asymmetry is the spine: organic is the engine that scales a region; the pack is a near-default proximity prize we still deliver but never center.

**Why the cap exists (mechanism, measured).**
- Google names Distance as one of three core local-ranking factors, alongside Relevance and Prominence ([support.google.com](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)). Proximity is a first-party, structural input — not vendor theory.
- The pack ranks on the verified physical address. The service-area setting is visual only and has no ranking impact ([sterlingsky.ca](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/does-the-service-area-in-google-my-business-impact-ranking/)). Listing a wide service area does not buy wide pack visibility.
- Google's November 2021 "Vicinity" update — its largest local change since 2017 — increased proximity weight and shrank the pack's radius of visibility, making it harder to rank outside the home city ([sterlingsky.ca](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/vicinity-algorithm-update/)). The cap is Google-imposed, not an artifact of weak SEO.

**How fast it decays (measured, n≈1,100 across 50 cities).**
- Pack rank follows exponential decay with a sharp first-mile cliff — an average drop of ~8 positions in the first mile ([rankings.io](https://rankings.io/data-studies/proximity-data-study/)).
- The effective radius is not a universal mile number. Top-20 exit rates by the edge of a 10-mile grid range from ~27% (Pittsburgh) to ~92% (Queens); dense metros sit ~80%+ (Boston 81.5%, LA 82.3%, NYC 80.8%) ([rankings.io](https://rankings.io/data-studies/proximity-data-study/)). This is why we promise a **floor**, not a fixed radius.

**What scaling the pack actually requires.**
- Reach is anchored to a point, so extending pack coverage to a region means adding staffed physical locations — one proximity bubble each ([brandonrollins.com](https://brandonrollins.com/p/how-to-rank-a-service-area-business-in-google)). The pack scales by capex/footprint. Concede this precisely: the cap is **per-location**, not company-wide. One office = one bubble.

**Why organic escapes it.**
- Google's organic algorithm is less proximity-constrained than Maps; organic city pages can rank in a city a business services but is not located in ([datapins.com](https://www.datapins.com/seo-for-multiple-cities/)). Sterling Sky's longitudinal study finds proximity is "less influential than in the past" on organic, and the pack is the more proximity-bound surface ([sterlingsky.ca](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/2025-update-how-proximity-impacts-google-organic-results/)).
- Scaling organic = content, on-page, authority — far cheaper per market than opening offices. That cost asymmetry is the offer argument.

**Where the pack still earns its keep.**
- The pack captures a large but **not dominant** share of clicks: ~29-32% pack vs ~44-51% organic in a controlled 5,500-tester study — organic out-clicks the pack ([brightlocal.com](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-services-ads-click-study/)). The pack is the highest-intent surface within radius, valuable for the home market and the emergency slice. Never call it useless.

**What NOT to oversell:** Do not assert a fixed "X-mile" radius, a precise proximity-weight percentage, or a hard company-wide revenue cap — the radius is density-dependent and directional, the weight is disputed and likely declining, and the per-location cap can be lifted with capex.

**Pillar guardrails — what NOT to oversell:**
- Do not state a fixed 'X-mile' effective radius — it is density-dependent (27% to 92% top-20 exit across metros) and directional, not a measured constant. Promise a floor, project a ceiling.
- Do not assert a single precise proximity-weight percentage (e.g. '55%'). The weight is from expert surveys (modeled/perceived), is disputed, and is likely declining; some 2025 sources put it near 15%. State the mechanism, not a number.
- Do not claim a hard company-wide revenue cap. The cap is per-LOCATION; it can be lifted with capex (new staffed offices, each a new proximity bubble). The argument is cost asymmetry, not impossibility.
- Do not call the pack useless or a pure non-scaling byproduct — it still takes ~29-44% of clicks and is the highest-intent surface within radius, especially for the home market and emergency slice. We win it on ties.
- Do not claim prominence/proximity coefficients from Google — Distance/Relevance/Prominence are named as factors, but no causal weights are published. Cite the factor, not a coefficient.
- Do not present single-metro or single-vertical studies (BrightLocal SF testers; Rankings.io law-firm keyword) as universal — label scope; they are directional outside their sample.
- Exclude any PBN / link-scheme / gray-hat path entirely; ongoing client-managed off-page is out of scope and must not be used to back-fill the radius-escape claim.

### Pillar 2 — Prominence dilates the pack radius (organic feeds the map)
## T2 — Prominence dilates the pack radius (organic feeds the map)

**Bottom line: web authority is a stated, first-party input to local-pack ranking — but it widens the effective radius, it does not remove the distance cap. We sell the engine (organic dominance) and deliver the byproduct (a proximity-capped pack lift). We never sell the site as the pack-winner.**

Google names three local ranking factors: relevance, distance, and prominence ([support.google.com](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en)). Prominence is defined first-party as "how well-known a business is," and Google states it is "based on info like how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have" ([support.google.com](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en)). So organic web authority is not a theory we invented — it is a ranking input Google publishes itself.

The mechanism is radius dilation. A business with stronger prominence and relevance signals than physically-closer competitors can outrank them and rank across a wider geographic radius ([searchengineland.com](https://searchengineland.com/guide/google-proximity-bias-in-local-search)). Practitioners call this "ranking radius" — a well-optimized site plus strong reputation lets a business compete farther from its physical address ([bigorangeplanet.com](https://www.bigorangeplanet.com/2026/05/17/how-proximity-impacts-local-rankings-in-google-search)). This is directional and mechanistic. There is no defensible miles-per-link figure — anyone quoting one is making it up.

The payoff is asymmetric, and the asymmetry is the whole offer. Web/link authority moves ORGANIC rankings strongly and the LOCAL PACK only weakly. The 2026 Whitespark Local Search Ranking Factors survey (47 experts, modeled weights) puts on-page signals near 33% in local organic versus ~15% in the local pack — roughly 2x heavier organically — with links a top-tier organic factor (~24%) but a minor pack one ([whitespark.ca](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/)). BrightLocal's read of the same data: link signals keep dropping, "particularly for local pack rankings" (~8% category weight) ([brightlocal.com](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/google-local-algorithm-and-ranking-factors/)). Because Whitespark is an expert-opinion survey (modeled, not measured), we corroborate it with measured field work: Joy Hawkins / Sterling Sky isolated link building and showed organic and pack rankings respond very differently — "links do not impact the organic + local algorithm the same" ([brightonseo.com](https://brightonseo.com/events/san-diego-2024/talks/does-link-building-actually-influence-local-pack-rankings)).

So the floor we promise and the ceiling we project:
- **Floor (defensible, always true):** organic authority is a published prominence input; building it widens the site's organic reach and supports the pack. Organic and local are independent surfaces — the site can own page-one organic outright while the pack is contested ([brightlocal.com](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/google-local-algorithm-and-ranking-factors/)).
- **Ceiling (directional, never a point-promise):** a more prominent business slightly farther out can outrank a closer competitor with weak signals ([searchengineland.com](https://searchengineland.com/guide/google-proximity-bias-in-local-search)) — which is exactly how organic dominance scales a brand past the ~5-mile pack radius that caps local revenue.

The hard counterweight, stated plainly: proximity still gates the pack. The best independent measurement of pack drivers (Sterling Sky, 8,186 businesses across 200 cities) found on-page content correlates only SLIGHTLY with pack ranking — review recency and a visible address dominated ([sterlingsky.ca](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/what-gets-you-ranking-for-near-me-2025/)). Pack results cluster in a tight several-mile radius even in huge metros ([sterlingsky.ca](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/local-pack-clustering-making-sense-of-googles-ranking-patterns/)), and hiding the address — removing the proximity anchor — tanked a real client's rankings and erased the pack for a keyword entirely ([sterlingsky.ca](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/hiding-address-google-business-ranking/)). Distance is a structural input the site cannot override. Prominence dilates the radius around a REAL office; it does not let a business rank anywhere regardless of location.

**What NOT to oversell:** never promise a measured radius gain, a prominence-beats-proximity percentage (the 60%/15% split is uncited agency lore), or that organic ranking "lifts pack rank by X" — the organic↔local correlation is marketer belief, not algorithmic causation. The site wins organic and supports the pack; proximity + review velocity win the pack, and we deliver those off-page, not with pages.

**Pillar guardrails — what NOT to oversell:**
- Never promise a measured radius gain or a miles-per-link figure — radius dilation is directional/mechanistic; no source quantifies it in distance.
- Do not use the '60% prominence / 15% proximity' split — it is uncited agency lore. The only defensible quantification is the Whitespark on-page asymmetry (~33% organic vs ~15% pack), and that is modeled/surveyed, not measured.
- Do not claim organic ranking 'lifts pack rank by X.' The 88% organic-local correlation is marketer BELIEF (survey), not algorithmic causation — keep it at 'organic feeds a stated prominence input.'
- Do not sell the site as the pack-winner. Proximity gates the pack (tight several-mile cluster; hiding the address erases it); review recency + visible address are the dominant pack levers, and those are off-page fulfillment, not pages.
- Label Whitespark weights 'modeled/surveyed' and always pair them with the measured Sterling Sky link-isolation case studies, or a skeptic dismisses the asymmetry as crowdsourced opinion.
- Do not assert organic-first is universal best practice — most local-SEO guidance prioritizes the pack for small local businesses. KM's organic-first emphasis rests specifically on the high-ticket/regional-scale argument (only organic scales past the ~5-mile pack radius), not on the pack being unimportant.
- Exclude all PBN / link-scheme / gray-hat mechanisms entirely — the prominence lift must come from legitimate one-time on-page authority and clean links only.

### Pillar 3 — Link-absorption capacity (deep site vs thin site)
## T3 — Link-Absorption Capacity: Deep Site vs Thin Site

**Bottom line.** A deep, well-architected site is an *absorptive structure*. It converts link investment into rankings more safely and more efficiently than a thin site — not because more pages rank by themselves, but because more genuine pages give link equity more places to land and anchor text more ways to vary. King Maker ships that structure once. The structure is the one-time asset; it does not earn the links by itself.

**Internal linking causally moves organic traffic — this is measured, not theory.** A controlled SEO split-test on a large site found that increasing internal linking produced a **25% uplift in organic traffic** across category pages, with both the linked-to and the linked-from page groups gaining 20%+ ([searchpilot.com](https://www.searchpilot.com/resources/case-studies/seo-split-test-lessons-increasing-internal-linking)). Separate split-tests show the same mechanism at smaller magnitudes: adding related-article links lifted donor pages ~16% (95% probability of a positive effect) ([searchpilot.com](https://www.searchpilot.com/resources/case-studies/seo-split-test-lessons-increasing-related-article-links)), expanding a homepage footer's link set lifted the linked-to pages ~5% ([searchpilot.com](https://www.searchpilot.com/resources/blog/3-seo-split-tests-you-should-try)), and — directly relevant to a multi-city roofing spine — linking to **nearby location pages lifted those location pages ~7%** ([searchpilot.com](https://www.searchpilot.com/resources/case-studies/seo-split-test-lessons-nearby-location-links)). These are split-tests, so they are causal. A 5-page site has almost no internal links to add; a 40-50+ URL silo can build this mesh across every service-and-city target.

**Why depth = capacity, mechanically.** Internal links distribute PageRank as a fraction per outbound link, decaying with each click-hop from an authority page ([visively.com](https://visively.com/kb/content/internal-linking-architecture)). Google's own spokesperson confirms internal linking is how a site signals which pages matter — bury a page and Google reads it as unimportant ([searchenginejournal.com](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-john-mueller-rank-important-pages/345192/)). A deep, siloed site can route equity down topical paths to many rankable targets and surface them shallowly. A thin site has nowhere to route it.

**Why depth = safety under link investment.** Google names "links with optimized anchor text" and "excessive link exchanges" as link spam in its own spam policy ([developers.google.com](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)). Risk is assessed *relationally* — across the whole anchor-text distribution and link velocity, not link-by-link. A thin site funneling links into a homepage on the money keyword is forced into anchor concentration; practitioner consensus puts safe exact-match commercial anchors at **under ~5-10% of the profile** ([basesearchmarketing.com](https://www.basesearchmarketing.com/blog/right-ratio-of-exact-match-anchor-text-in-link-building/)), and a sudden same-anchor spike is itself a flagged pattern ([searchengineland.com](https://searchengineland.com/guide/link-velocity)). A deep spine spreads the same link budget across many topical URLs with varied, natural anchors — landing as diversity instead of stuffing.

**What the downside actually is — floor, not catastrophe.** Google's enforcement default against unnatural links is *devaluation*: SpamBrain neutralizes the credit so the gain disappears, which is distinct from a manual penalty (Dec 2022 link spam update; the "neutralize the impact of unnatural links" framing is corroborated via SE Roundtable / Authority Builders coverage of the update — [developers.google.com](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2022/12/december-22-link-spam-update)). So the honest claim is: a thin site **wastes** an aggressive link budget (devalued) and carries elevated demotion *tail*-risk — not a guaranteed competitor penalty. Sell the efficiency and safety, not a collapse.

**Floor vs ceiling.** *Floor (promise):* a deep silo of genuine pages routes link equity to many rankable targets and keeps exact-match anchors diluted across the profile — safer and more efficient absorption of any given link budget. *Ceiling (project, directional):* internal-mesh changes of this kind have produced 5-25% organic uplifts in controlled tests; vertical sources put on-page service-and-location clarity at ~36% of local *organic* weight (directional, agency-sourced, lineage to Whitespark/Moz survey work — not Google-confirmed).

**Hard boundary (load-bearing honesty).** Depth only converts to absorption capacity if every page is genuinely unique and valuable — Google explicitly rejects "more pages = more ranking" ([searchenginejournal.com](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-john-mueller-rank-important-pages/345192/)). A doorway-padded deep site fails the same way a thin site does. The asset is *depth of genuine pages + silo architecture*, gated by the real-job→page / delete-the-city-name test.

**What NOT to oversell:** never "thin sites get penalized" (devaluation is the base case); never a dollar figure or page count as a rank guarantee; never claim the site is the dominant local lever (GBP/reviews outweigh it for the pack); and the one-time deliverable is the absorptive *structure*, not the off-page links themselves.

**Pillar guardrails — what NOT to oversell:**
- Never claim 'thin sites get penalized' — Google's default enforcement against unnatural links is DEVALUATION (credit neutralized via SpamBrain), not a manual penalty. Frame it as wasted budget + elevated tail risk, never a guaranteed competitor collapse.
- Never state 'more pages = more rank.' Google explicitly rejects page count as a ranking mechanism; depth only converts to link-absorption capacity if every page is genuinely unique/valuable (real-job->page, delete-the-city-name gate). A doorway-padded deep site fails like a thin one.
- Never present a dollar figure ($5-10k/mo absorption) as a rank guarantee. It is a MECHANISM claim — a 40-50+ URL spine gives ~10x the anchor-distribution headroom of a 5-page site, so a larger budget lands as natural diversity instead of homepage anchor stuffing. Mechanism, not outcome.
- Never imply the SearchPilot causal evidence covers off-page link acquisition. The clean causal proof is for INTERNAL links (fully site-controlled). External link earning — where velocity/over-optimization risk actually lives — is ongoing off-page work, out of scope. The one-time deliverable is the absorptive structure, not the earned links.
- Never claim the site is the dominant LOCAL lever. Home-services sources consistently put GBP (~32% of Map Pack) and reviews (~20%) above on-page for the pack; the ~36% on-page figure is for local ORGANIC weight, is agency-sourced/directional, and traces to Whitespark/Moz survey lineage — not Google-confirmed. The pack stays a proximity-capped byproduct.
- Treat exact-match anchor thresholds (<5-10% safe; >30% flag) and the per-hop equity math as practitioner-modeled/directional, not Google-confirmed numbers. Label them directional on any slide.
- Deep alone is NOT automatically better — more links also dilute per-link equity. The claim is 'deep AND well-architected silo (topical silos, body-text contextual links, controlled counts),' which is the actual deliverable. A sprawling unstructured page-dump leaks equity too.

### Pillar 4 — Map-vs-organic ranking-factor weighting
## T4 — Map-vs-Organic Ranking-Factor Weighting

**Bottom line:** Local rank splits into two different games with two different scoreboards. In **Local Organic**, on-page content plus links carry the majority of modeled ranking weight — about 57% combined (on-page 33%, links 24%), while the Google Business Profile is only ~7% ([BrightLocal 2026](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/google-local-algorithm-and-ranking-factors/), [AdviceLocal summary of Whitespark 2026](https://www.advicelocal.com/blog/2026-local-search-ranking-factors-maps-organic-ai/)). In the **Local Pack (Maps)**, the scoreboard flips: GBP plus reviews carry ~52% (GBP 32%, reviews 20%), on-page drops to 15%, links to 8% ([AdviceLocal](https://www.advicelocal.com/blog/2026-local-search-ranking-factors-maps-organic-ai/), corroborated by [Kyle Eggleston](https://www.kyleeggleston.com/blog/2026-local-seo-ranking-factors/)). A deep site plus one-time shippable off-page directly moves the levers that dominate organic. It does not move the levers that dominate the pack — those are gated by proximity, profile, and review velocity. So the engine of regional dominance is organic; the pack is a byproduct we capture inside the office radius but never center.

**The pack is proximity-capped — that is the structural ceiling, not an opinion.** A measured XGBoost study on ~3,200 businesses (R² ~92-93%) found proximity accounts for 55.2% of GBP visibility across positions 1-21, dropping to 36.2% in the top 10 where reviews and content relevance take over ([Search Atlas study, via secondary write-up](https://infinityrank.medium.com/local-seo-just-got-a-map-update-new-study-quantifies-what-actually-drives-google-business-profile-be4338ea1e52)). Proximity weight also varies sharply by vertical (law 69.4%, food ~30%), so any flat "proximity = X%" claim is wrong; cite the range, not a point. Sterling Sky's measured study (8,186 businesses, 200 cities) independently confirms that hiding your address correlates negatively with pack rank, and that this-month review velocity beats total review count ([Sterling Sky](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/what-gets-you-ranking-for-near-me-2025/)). Translation: no amount of site depth overcomes a closer competitor's geography in the pack. That is why we do not sell the pack as a site-driven win.

**On-page still helps even on the pack, which is why the deep site compounds.** Sterling Sky measured that landing pages with "more meaningful words" (substance, not volume) correlate with better pack rank ([Sterling Sky](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/what-gets-you-ranking-for-near-me-2025/)). So the deep site lifts the proximity-capped pack at the margin and wins organic outright — it works on both surfaces, harder on the one that scales.

**Click-reality check — promise the floor, not a fantasy.** In 2025, ~44% of local-search clicks went to the pack vs ~29% organic, ~19% paid ([Monday Digital](https://mondaydigital.com/blog/google-local-pack-traffic-statistics/)). The pack wins the plurality head-to-head — we do not pretend otherwise. The case for organic is scale and control: the pack is capped to one office radius and flat across the top 3 (CTR #1 17.8% / #2 15.4% / #3 15.1% — only ~3 points spread; [Monday Digital](https://mondaydigital.com/blog/google-local-pack-traffic-statistics/)), while organic scales region-wide. Zero-click is real and rising (~68% of U.S. searches end without a click, [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-zero-click-searches-2026-study-479717)) — but it de-centers the pack too, and Whitespark's third 2026 column shows AI-search visibility is won by the on-page + citations + links bundle (on-page 24%, GBP only 12%; [AdviceLocal](https://www.advicelocal.com/blog/2026-local-search-ranking-factors-maps-organic-ai/)). As clicks erode, durable visibility shifts further toward the deep site, not away from it.

**What NOT to oversell:** these splits are a modeled expert-opinion survey (47 experts, 187 factors; [Sam Bretzmann summary](https://sambretzmann.com/2026-local-seo-ranking-factors-overview/)), not Google-published weights — cite the direction (which levers dominate which surface), never the decimal as certified. Do not claim the pack is "60-90%" of clicks (it is ~44%). Do not promise pack top-3 (proximity-gated). Do not promise raw organic click-volume growth in a zero-click world — promise organic visibility/dominance across the region. Never PBNs or link schemes. Ongoing client-managed off-page is out of scope.

**Pillar guardrails — what NOT to oversell:**

### Pillar 5 — Map-pack EARLY-STAGE reality: the honest infiltration math
## T5 — Map-Pack Early-Stage Reality: The Honest Infiltration Math

**Bottom line: the map pack is a low, slow-maturing channel at launch — not a couple jobs a week, not ten retail jobs out of the gate.** A new profile is penalized twice on day one: it is closer-competitor-capped to a small proximity bubble, and it loses clicks to higher-review competitors even at equal or better rank. Both penalties decay only over months as reviews accumulate. This is why King Maker centers **organic regional dominance** and treats the pack as a proximity-capped byproduct we deliver but never headline.

**Penalty 1 — proximity caps the pack to a bubble, and it is the one lever the business cannot directly pull.** Distance to the searcher is the dominant factor Google uses to assemble the pack ([whitespark.ca](https://whitespark.ca/guides/how-to-rank-in-surrounding-cities-or-towns-every-local-seo-strategy-you-need-to-know/)). A business sitting just outside a searcher's perimeter may not be considered relevant at all ([whitespark.ca](https://whitespark.ca/guides/how-to-rank-in-surrounding-cities-or-towns-every-local-seo-strategy-you-need-to-know/)). On geo-grid tools this shows as the "Proximity Halo" — green (top-3) at the office, fading to red two miles out, because a closer competitor wins each distant grid point ([localdominator.co](https://localdominator.co/what-is-geogrid-rank-tracker/)). The honest metric is therefore Share of Local Voice — the percentage of grid points where you rank top-3 — not a single "we rank #1 in [town]" check measured from the office pin ([localfalcon.com](https://www.localfalcon.com/blog/how-local-falcon-measures-your-local-rankings-and-visibility-across-ai-platforms-and-google-maps)). A fresh profile that honestly wins its own address can still have a low SoLV across the town.

**Penalty 2 — review asymmetry skips clicks past a thin profile even when it ranks.** In a controlled first-click test, pack position did not cleanly determine clicks — #1 won 14%, #2 won 12%, #3 collapsed to 1% — and the most-cited reason participants gave for their click was reviews and business longevity ([ilawyermarketing.com](https://www.ilawyermarketing.com/click-test-study-lsa-local-pack-organic-google-serps/)). BrightLocal's click study independently confirms the #3 cliff: position 3 took only 2.3–2.9% of clicks versus ~16–17% for #1 ([brightlocal.com](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-services-ads-click-study/)). Star rating moves clicks independent of position — a 5-star listing earns ~25% more pack clicks than a 3-star one ([brightlocal.com](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/review-search-click-through-study/)). A new profile with no rating loses clicks even when it ranks.

**The maturation curve.** A new GBP matures over months, not instantly. The most defensible single early milestone is ~10 reviews: across three businesses, Sterling Sky observed a small but noticeable Maps ranking bump exactly at the 9→10 review crossing — but no further bump at 10→11 or 16→31, so review count is a threshold effect, not a linear lever ([sterlingsky.ca](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/number-of-reviews-impact-ranking/)). A visible (not hidden) address and recent review volume are independently correlated ranking factors in the largest dataset — 8,186 businesses across 200 cities ([sterlingsky.ca](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/what-gets-you-ranking-for-near-me-2025/)). Practitioner consensus puts measurable movement at ~3–6 months, 6–12 in competitive markets — directional, not a controlled study ([sterlingsky.ca](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/what-gets-you-ranking-for-near-me-2025/)).

**Why organic is the bigger surface.** BrightLocal found organic results out-click the pack: 50.8% organic vs 32.3% pack with no Local Services Ads present, 43.9% vs 28.8% with them ([brightlocal.com](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-services-ads-click-study/)). Organic position 1 alone (25.5–27.4%) out-pulls the entire pack #1 slot ([brightlocal.com](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-services-ads-click-study/)). Organic is not capped to one office pin, so it is the larger, more scalable reach surface — the strategic reason the offer centers it.

**The honest job-shape (MODELED, not promised).** Composing the measured inputs — #3 pack CTR of 2.3–2.9% ([brightlocal.com](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-services-ads-click-study/)), the review-driven click-skip, and the months-long ~10-review maturation gate — the early pack yield is a low curve in the range of ~1–2 jobs per month, maturing slowly toward ~10/month only after roughly two years with matched reviews and an expanded grid. **These are illustrative ranges built on measured CTR and review inputs, not a fixed count we promise.** Promise the floor (the proximity bubble); project the ceiling (an expanded grid) — never sell the ceiling out of the gate.

**One caveat to expect.** The skeptic's strongest card is the "44% click the map pack vs 29% organic" stat waved across vendor blogs as current. It traces to Moz's 2015 Snack Pack study — ~11 years old, pre-modern-SERP, and methodologically non-comparable to the BrightLocal click studies above. Both can be true: the pack wins clicks *when it appears for the proximity-bubble searcher*; organic wins the larger distance-unconstrained surface. Don't rest the pillar on "organic always out-clicks the pack" as settled.

**What NOT to oversell:** the pack is low and slow early — never a couple jobs a week out of the gate, never a roofing-specific click-to-job math, never the pack ranking number as the headline win. No PBNs, link schemes, or gray-hat — excluded explicitly. Off-page review/citation work that is ongoing client-managed fulfillment is out of scope here.

**Pillar guardrails — what NOT to oversell:**
- The early pack channel is LOW and SLOW — never 'a couple jobs a week' or '10 retail jobs out of the gate'; the ~1-2 jobs/mo early and ~10/mo @ ~2yr figures are MODELED illustrative ranges, not promised counts.
- Do NOT present pack CTR numbers as roofing-specific job math — both clean datasets are legal/SF service searches on mock SERPs; use them as SHAPE (front-loaded, review-modulated), directional for roofing only.
- Do NOT assert a clean 'review count overrides position' percentage from the +25%/+39% BrightLocal study — it is 2016, mock-SERP, and varied STAR RATING, not review COUNT; frame as a star-rating/social-proof effect.
- Do NOT treat 'organic always out-clicks the pack' as settled — the stale 2015 Moz '44% pack' stat is the skeptic's strongest card; reconcile (pack wins WHEN it appears for the bubble searcher; organic wins the larger surface), don't ignore it.
- Do NOT cite the '55% proximity' point-figure — it never traced to a primary Whitespark table; assert proximity is the dominant factor qualitatively without the percent.
- Label the 3-6 mo / 6-12 mo maturation timeline as practitioner-consensus/modeled, not a controlled study; lead with the measured 10-review threshold and address-visibility correlation as the hard anchors.
- EXCLUDE explicitly: PBNs, link schemes, gray-hat. Ongoing client-managed off-page is out of scope; only one-time shippable off-page is in scope.
- Promise the FLOOR (proximity bubble), project the CEILING (expanded grid via relevance/prominence) — never sell the expanded-grid ceiling out of the gate.

### Pillar 6 — The sequencing: organic carries the pipeline (months ~1–18)
## T6 — The Sequencing: Organic Carries the Pipeline (Months ~1–18)

**Bottom line:** Organic is the day-one and sustained engine. The map pack is a proximity-capped byproduct we deliver but never center. We build the website to carry the pipeline while the pack matures on its own slower, review-gated clock. This is a strategy SHAPE, not a dated rank guarantee.

**Why organic is the engine, not the second channel.** In a controlled local-SERP click study, organic results took the most clicks of any SERP type — 50.8% with no Local Services Ads present, 43.9% with LSAs present — ahead of the local pack at 32.3% / 28.8% ([brightlocal.com](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-services-ads-click-study/)). Organic is not the smaller channel it is often assumed to be; it is the larger one.

**Why organic reaches the whole town and the pack cannot.** The local pack fractures geographically. Every pin on a geo-grid is a separate request from a distinct lat/long, so the same business can rank #1 at its office and fall to roughly #7–#10 a few blocks away ([brightlocal.com](https://www.brightlocal.com/resources/geo-grid-ranking-tool-comparison-guide/)). City-intent organic does not fracture this way. In a 549-search / 61-pin study, once a city modifier was added the top-5 organic positions held constant across every test pin ([sterlingsky.ca](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/2025-update-how-proximity-impacts-google-organic-results/)). A town-named `/locations/[city]` page therefore serves the whole town on the same terms regardless of where the searcher stands — the location-independent reach the pack structurally cannot deliver. This is the documented way one website covers a metro of surrounding cities where a service-area business has no physical address and is "extremely unlikely" to appear in the 3-pack ([sangfroidwebdesign.com](https://www.sangfroidwebdesign.com/local-seo/city-pages/)).

**Why the website is the lever for organic — and only a partial lever for the pack.** The two channels run on different ranking-factor weights. BrightLocal's local-algorithm breakdown puts Local Organic at On-page 33% / Links 24% / Behavioral 10%, versus Local Pack at Google Business Profile 32% / Reviews 20% / On-page 15% ([brightlocal.com](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/google-local-algorithm-and-ranking-factors/)). A well-built site (on-page + one-time links) moves organic directly; the pack is gated on GBP completeness and review accumulation. Google's own docs confirm the channels are entangled, not separate: "Your position in web results is also a factor, so search engine optimization (SEO) best practices apply" ([support.google.com](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)). Organic is the leading edge of the same engine — as organic authority lands it pulls the pack up over time. We do not claim the pack is dead weight for 18 months; it begins maturing as organic prominence accrues.

**The honest ramp — floor and ceiling.** Organic compounds; it is not instant. Only 1.74% of newly published pages reach Google's top 10 within a year, and the average #1-ranking page is five years old ([ahrefs.com](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-rank-in-google-and-how-old-are-top-ranking-pages/)). That is the head-term reality, and we never promise around it. But the same study shows 40.82% of pages that do reach the top 10 get there within one month ([ahrefs.com](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-rank-in-google-and-how-old-are-top-ranking-pages/)) — the mechanism by which lower-competition, long-tail, town-named pages can rank in weeks-to-months. So:
- **FLOOR (promise this):** long-tail and city-intent pages move within months. Roofing-vertical agency consensus reports ranking movement typically within 3–6 months ([gomarketing.com](https://gomarketing.com/blog/how-roofing-companies-rank-1-in-local-google-search-in-2026/)) — directional, vendor-incentive, label it as such.
- **CEILING (project, do not promise):** head-term regional dominance compounds over ~6–18 months as authority accrues.

**Why organic must carry months 1–18.** The pack is the slow, maintenance-heavy channel it has to wait on. Agency consensus puts competitive top-3 map-pack placement at 6–12 months of consistent effort, with reviews compounding rather than boosting instantly ([bluemonkfish.com](https://bluemonkfish.com/how-long-does-local-seo-take/)) — and positions decay over 2–4 months without ongoing review/freshness signals (same source; directional, agency-sourced, not measured). Organic ranking, once earned, is the more stable, town-wide, durable spine. That is the sequencing: organic carries the pipeline while the pack heals and matures underneath it.

**What NOT to oversell:** Do not claim "organic ignores proximity." The measured, defensible claim is narrow — a *city-modified / explicit-intent* organic query ranks stably across the town; bare "near me" / implicit queries still shift, and Sterling Sky's own 2022 data showed proximity then hit organic *harder* than the pack ([sterlingsky.ca](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/2025-update-how-proximity-impacts-google-organic-results/)). Sell the SHAPE and the FLOOR, never a month-X rank guarantee.

**Pillar guardrails — what NOT to oversell:**
- Do NOT claim 'organic ignores proximity' or is 'proximity-immune.' The measured claim is narrow: a CITY-MODIFIED / explicit-intent organic query ranks stably across the town. Bare 'near me' / implicit organic queries still shift by searcher location, and Sterling Sky's own 2022 study found proximity then affected organic MORE strongly than the pack. Frame it as 'a town-named organic page ranks uniformly across the town' — never the blanket version, which is falsifiable.
- Do NOT present '1–18 months' as a dated rank guarantee. The data (1.74% of new pages reach top-10 in a year; average #1 page is 5 years old) does not support a promise that organic WILL rank in that window for competitive head terms. Sell the SHAPE and the FLOOR (long-tail / low-competition city pages rank in weeks-to-months — 40.82% of rankers within a month); project the head-term ceiling as a 6–18mo+ compounding outcome, not a month-X promise.
- Label the local-SEO-timeline numbers (6–12mo pack maturation, 2–4mo decay) as directional agency/vendor consensus, NOT measured research — bluemonkfish is an agency blog with sales incentive. They are corroborated by the independent BrightLocal review-weight findings, but present the month-counts as 'ranges agencies typically report.'
- Flag the roofing 3–6mo movement figure as vendor-incentive (gomarketing is a roofing agency). Use it only as a directional vertical illustration, never as measured proof of timeline.
- Do NOT imply the pack is dead weight or inert for 18 months. Google's first-party docs say organic position feeds pack prominence — so the pack begins maturing as organic authority accrues. The honest frame is 'organic is the leading edge that drags the pack up,' not two fully separate tracks.
- Keep ONGOING client-managed off-page (review-velocity programs, monthly link-building) OUT of scope — it is not what this pillar promises. The pillar is about what the SHIPPED website (on-page + one-time links + city pages) does for organic. Do not pivot the sequencing story to GBP/review ops.

### Pillar 7 — The organic lead-QUALITY differential (scoped to high-ticket)
## T7 — The Organic Lead-Quality Differential (High-Ticket Only)

**Bottom line.** For considered, high-ticket roofing jobs ($15-30k), an organic-owned visitor is a better lead than a map-pack lead — but not because organic "converts at a higher rate." At the raw session level, organic and paid search convert almost identically (organic 4.8% vs paid 5.1% in Construction & Engineering, across 110M+ sessions tracked) ([Ruler Analytics](https://www.ruleranalytics.com/blog/insight/conversion-rate-by-industry/)). The edge is four mechanisms — intent split, ticket size, owned environment, and de-risking — not a conversion-rate number. Lead with the mechanisms; never lead with "organic converts higher."

**The intent split.** The pack is a side-by-side, three-pin commodity surface with one-tap call buttons — it structurally feeds comparison-shopping ([Tara Saka](https://tarasaka.com/google-maps-seo-for-roofers/)). Considered buyers run a bake-off: ~70% of homeowners compare 2-3 quotes, ~85% compare three or fewer, and ~65% call pricing "very important" ([Modernize 2024](https://modernize.com/homeowner-insights/contractor-selection-trends)). A visitor who navigated to one brand's owned site has already partially self-selected out of the side-by-side. This does not *eliminate* the bake-off — even considered buyers are price-aware — it improves win probability by letting the brand frame value before price enters.

**The owned environment.** High-ticket buyers research a brand's public presence — content, portfolio, evidence of results — before they make contact ([practitioner; high-ticket psychology](https://medium.com/@tiffygwrites/the-quiet-lurker-buyer-understanding-high-ticket-buyer-psychology-01d2f8e57d37)). They weigh value and outcomes over lowest price and research for weeks ([Spear](https://www.getspear.ai/blog-post/high-ticket-sales-a-playbook-of-strategies-to-win-the-game)). An organic visitor converts on the brand's E-E-A-T-rich turf, not in a multi-pin grid. Caveat: high-ticket buyers are *more* demanding, not less — the owned site has to actually demonstrate authority for this to pay.

**The intent-quality gradient (directional support).** Self-initiated, intent-rich contact closes far better than interruptive contact: referral close rates run above 50%, ad-response under 20% ([Hook benchmark]). Exclusive high-intent contractor leads close 23% at $8,400 (roofing) and 31% at $22,000 (windows) versus a shared-lead floor of ~6-10% ([LeadGen Jay](https://leadgenjay.com/blog/best-lead-services-for-contractors-2026-7-expert-tested-winners)). The much-restated "14.6% inbound vs 1.7% outbound" pair is directional color only — it is inbound-vs-outbound, not organic-vs-pack ([restated benchmark](https://blog.nimblo.ai/enterprise-seo-lead-generation-14-percent-close-rate/)). None of these is a direct organic-vs-pack measurement; they establish the general principle organic-owned leads exploit.

**De-risking.** A map pin is a rented asset. GBP suspension reports surged 80%+ since 2023 ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-business-profile-suspensions-increase-40169.html)), and appeal wait times ran 5-6 weeks in early 2025 with no guaranteed reinstatement ([Sterling Sky](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/top-reasons-google-my-business-suspended-your-listing/)). This is a real, rising risk with a multi-week recovery — measured, not epidemic. **Do not assert "35% of profiles suspended yearly"** — that figure traces to no primary source. An owned domain carries no such suspension exposure.

**The cost curve.** Once a domain ranks, organic CPL floors at roughly $10-90 versus paid search ~$165 and roofing LSA ~$60-120, and effective CPL *drops* as the asset matures — the inverse of rented channels ([benchmark range](https://www.estatehub.io/articles/2026-benchmarks-lead-conversion-rates-home-services)). Promise the floor; project the maturity curve as the ceiling.

**Two hard conditions on all of the above.** (1) The differential is **conditional on actually ranking for money terms** — ~90% of pages get zero Google traffic, and most SEO fails by targeting informational not commercial queries ([Marketing Eye](https://www.marketingeye.com/blog/why-most-seo-strategies-fail-to-generate-leads.html)). The quality differential is a *consequence of ranking*, never automatic from "having a good site" — which is why the offer must be tied to a ranking outcome. (2) It is **scoped to considered/high-ticket only.** For urgent jobs (active leak, storm), pack speed wins — first responders get ~5x the hire rate ([Thumbtack via LeadGen Jay](https://leadgenjay.com/blog/best-lead-services-for-contractors-2026-7-expert-tested-winners)). And organic takes 6-12 months to mature, so the build period needs a bridge for near-term volume.

**What NOT to oversell:** Do not claim organic "converts at a higher rate" (it converts at parity with paid) or quantify "arrives pre-sold" (the ~70% figure is modeled, not measured); the owned site improves win probability in a bake-off, it does not erase the bake-off.

**Pillar guardrails — what NOT to oversell:**
- Never claim 'organic leads convert at a higher rate' as a flat fact — independent session data shows organic and paid convert at parity (4.8% vs 5.1% in Construction & Engineering). The edge is intent-mix, ticket, owned environment, and de-risking, not rate.
- Do not quantify 'arrives pre-sold ~70%' — that number is modeled, not measured. The owned site improves win probability in a bake-off; it does not eliminate the bake-off. Even considered buyers compare 2-3 quotes and ~65% call price 'very important.'
- Do not assert '35% of GBP profiles suspended yearly' or any precise annual base rate — it traces to no primary source. Say suspensions are a real, rising risk with a multi-week, non-guaranteed appeal.
- Do not claim organic > pack universally. It is true for considered $15-30k jobs; for urgent/emergency jobs the pack's response-speed dynamic wins (first responder ~5x hire rate).
- Do not promise the differential as automatic from 'having a good site' — ~90% of pages get zero Google traffic. It is a consequence of ranking for money terms; tie the offer to a ranking outcome.
- Do not present the '14.6% vs 1.7%' inbound stat or the exclusive-lead close rates as organic-vs-pack proof — both are directional/principle support only (inbound-vs-outbound and exclusive-vs-shared respectively).
- Do not sell organic as immediate — it takes 6-12 months to mature; the build period needs a paid/pack bridge for near-term volume.

### Pillar 8 — Zero-pack long-tail volume (organic-only territory)
## T8 — Zero-Pack Long-Tail Volume (Organic-Only Territory)

**Bottom line: roughly 70% of searches return no map pack at all, and the pack-free majority is research/cost/comparison territory a thin pack-optimized site is structurally blind to.** A deep `/resources` + cost-guide + glossary silo is the only business-owned asset eligible to compete there. We promise eligibility and authority on that territory — not a traffic windfall. That distinction is the whole defensibility of this pillar.

**The pack owns the bottom funnel. We never contest it.** In a June 2026 SERP study of UK trades, the roofing vertical showed a local pack on 97.4% of genuine hire-intent queries — but only after research/cost/comparison/jobs/training/brand "noise" was filtered out of the keyword set ([studio36digital.co.uk](https://studio36digital.co.uk/uk-local-seo-statistics-2026/)). That filtered-out slice is exactly where the silos play. The two are separate demand pools, not competitors for the same SERP. "Roofer near me" and "[city] roof repair" belong to the pack; "metal vs asphalt roof cost" and "how to file a roof insurance claim" do not.

**The non-pack territory is large, not niche.** Across a 1,000-keyword Ahrefs panel (22 industries, ~404M monthly searches, Oct 2025), only 29.7% of searches triggered a Local Pack — so roughly 70% returned none ([studio36digital.co.uk](https://studio36digital.co.uk/98-7-of-uk-searches-display-serp-features/)). Google's own long-cited figure is that ~46% of searches have local intent, implying ~54% do not ([seroundtable.com](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-46-of-searches-have-local-intent-26529.html)). Both are directional for US roofing — the 29.7% is a UK all-vertical average and the 46% is a 2018 Google statement, not a fresh measurement — so we anchor the offer on the measured intent split below, not on a precise roofing pack-trigger percentage.

**The measured anchor: a thin site has no eligible content to rank on research queries.** BrightLocal's controlled study (8,000 results, 800 manual searches, 20 verticals × 20 US cities) found business websites appear in 61% of top-10 organic results for *transactional* queries but only 32% for *informational* ones — where directories (37%) and content sites outrank them ([brightlocal.com](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/business-listings-visibility-study/)). The way to surface on research queries is deep informational content. A pack-optimized transactional site has nothing to enter. Firsthand SERP checks confirm the shape: "roof replacement cost" and "metal vs asphalt roof" return editorial guides and calculators — This Old House, Angi, the JLC Cost-vs-Value Report — with no map pack ([thisoldhouse.com](https://www.thisoldhouse.com/roofing/shingles-vs-metal-roof-cost)); "how to file a roof insurance claim" returns manufacturer and .gov authority pages — GAF, Texas TDI ([gaf.com](https://www.gaf.com/en-us/plan-design/roofing-insurance-claims), [tdi.texas.gov](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/tips/replacing-your-roof.html)).

**The honest counter-pressure: AI Overviews are eating this lane hardest.** AIO coverage ran ~6.5% of queries in Jan 2025, peaked near 25% mid-year, and settled ~16% by Nov — and ~91% of early-2025 AIOs were informational-intent ([searchengineland.com](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-surge-pullback-data-466314), corroborating the Semrush 10M-keyword dataset). ### Pillar 9 — The two execution conditions
## T9 — The Two Execution Conditions

**Bottom line.** Organic regional dominance is not bought, it is built — and it rests on two client-executed conditions King Maker installs as systems but does not run forever: (1) a **universal, unscreened review-velocity machine**, and (2) a **legitimate, earn-don't-buy off-page authority program** seeded by the one-time on-site link architecture we ship. The two move different surfaces and are not interchangeable. Reviews are the fast lever; off-page links are the slow compounding engine. Centering organic is correct on the math: with no Local Services Ads present, organic takes 50.8% of local-search clicks versus 32.3% for the map pack (https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-services-ads-click-study/). The pack is a real but proximity-capped minority — a byproduct we deliver, never the headline.

**Why the two conditions are not the same lever.** In Whitespark's 2026 model, review signals are ~20% of the pack but only ~8% of local organic, while link signals are ~24% of organic versus ~8% of the pack (https://thestacc.com/blog/local-seo-ranking-factors/). So a review mandate is primarily a pack-deficit healer; an off-page link program is primarily the organic-dominance engine. Treat those weights as directional ratios from an expert survey, not measured coefficients — they argue direction, not magnitude.

**Condition 1 — Review velocity (the fast lever).** Recency, not raw count, is what moves and protects ranking. Review recency rose from ranking factor #93 in 2023 to #11 in 2026, the single largest positional jump in Whitespark's study (https://thestacc.com/blog/local-seo-ranking-factors/). Sterling Sky's case-study tracking shows a noticeable bump around the 10-review mark and warns rankings can decline after roughly three weeks of dormancy (https://www.sterlingsky.ca/number-of-reviews-impact-ranking/) — small-sample observation, so we frame it as "sustained velocity protects ranking; dormancy risks decline," not a guaranteed drop/recovery curve. The mandate must be **universal and unscreened** to be legal: the FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule took effect Oct 21, 2024, carries civil penalties up to $51,744 per violation, and bans review suppression, incentivized reviews, and gating (https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials). King Maker ships an ask-everyone system — never a happy-customer filter.

**Condition 2 — Off-page authority (the slow compounding engine).** Links have a demonstrated causal effect on organic, not just correlation: Ahrefs disavowed 3,476 links to three of its own pages and watched traffic fall 13.3%–18%, recovering to ~99% when the disavow was removed (https://ahrefs.com/blog/impact-of-links/). The **one-time, in-scope** piece King Maker ships directly is the on-site link architecture: SearchPilot split-tests show +5% to +25% organic uplift, and a roofing-relevant test adding links to the 6 nearest geographic regions across ~8,000 location pages produced +7% organic uplift on the linked pages (https://www.searchpilot.com/resources/case-studies/seo-split-test-lessons-nearby-location-links). Ongoing earned links are client-managed fulfillment and out of scope — we build and instrument the machine; the client feeds it. **Honest timeline:** first impact ~8–10 weeks after acquisition, full compounding over 6–12 months (https://outreachz.com/blog/link-building-results-timeline/). No PBNs, no link schemes, no gray-hat — excluded explicitly.

SE Ranking's survey of 260 agencies: 64% charge under $1k/mo, 20% $1–2k, 13% $2–5k, only ~2% above $5k (https://seranking.com/blog/seo-pricing/). Ahrefs' poll of 439 providers corroborates: ~$3,209/mo average, 68.8% at $2k/mo or less (https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-pricing/). So the **floor** is a legitimate program at the typical $1–2k/mo; the **ceiling** is $5–10k/mo for genuine enterprise regional domination. The pillar SEED's "~19% of providers at $5k+/mo" does not survive the primary distribution — the honest figure is ~2%.

**The mechanism that links the two conditions.** Google's prominence framework treats backlink authority and web presence as signals that can let a business outrank closer competitors in the pack (https://thestacc.com/blog/local-seo-ranking-factors/). That is how an organic-first program indirectly lifts the pack — indirectly. Do not over-claim a direct pack-weight from links; links are only ~8% of the pack model.

**What NOT to oversell.** The Whitespark weights are expert-survey opinion, not causal coefficients — never quantify rank movement from them. The Sterling Sky cliff is small-sample, not a published magnitude. The off-page timeline means neither condition reliably moves organic by day 90 — tie any 90-day bar to the fast levers (review velocity, GBP, on-site ship) and let links back the 6–12mo claim. **The link is real and it follows (measured, not assumed).** A live GAF Master Elite profile's "Visit Website" anchor points to the contractor's own .com, carries `rel="noopener noreferrer"` with **no nofollow**, and sits in the server-rendered HTML (195,055 bytes, crawlable — not JS-injected) ([gaf.com](https://www.gaf.com/en-us/roofing-contractors/residential/usa/mo/kearney/elite-master-contractors-1119929)). A live Owens Corning profile is even cleaner — the contractor's outbound link carries `rel="(none)"`, a plain follow link, also in server-rendered HTML ([owenscorning.com](https://www.owenscorning.com/en-us/roofing/contractors/contractor-profile/219549)). These two are the load-bearing, measured-dofollow directories.

**CertainTeed is NOT confirmed — do not assume parity.** CertainTeed's Find-a-Pro is a JavaScript SPA with no server-rendered profile anchors exposed; per-profile dofollow is unverified and must be inspected on the specific live profile before we count it ([certainteed.com](https://www.certainteed.com/find-a-pro)). NRCA / association directories are gated dynamic-search (eWeb) — topically relevant but unverified equity, secondary tier ([industry.nrca.net](https://industry.nrca.net/eweb/DynamicPage.aspx?site=nrca&webcode=NRCAMbrDirectory)).

**Dofollow link equity, not just a citation.** The value is three-part: (1) a crawlable follow link feeding Google's links/prominence signal, (2) topical relevance (a roofing manufacturer linking a roofer), and (3) a homeowner referral channel off the directory itself ([localroofingseo.agency](https://www.localroofingseo.agency/blog/link-building-for-roofing-contractors)). We sell it on relevance + confirmed dofollow + referral — never on a borrowed DA/DR number, because Google does not use Moz DA or Ahrefs DR as ranking factors ([thehoth.com](https://www.thehoth.com/blog/da-vs-dr/)). We could not pull a numeric DR (checkers were CAPTCHA-gated); authority is substantiated qualitatively — GAF is the largest North American roofing manufacturer at ~40% share, founded 1886 ([goingwithgibbs.com](https://goingwithgibbs.com/gaf-vs-certainteed-vs-owens-corning-which-manufacturer-certification-matters-most/)).

**The link is credential-gated — qualify the prospect first.** It cannot be manufactured; the client must hold and maintain the cert. GAF Master Elite: <3% (as low as ~2%) of US roofers, requires licensure in good standing, ~$1M insurance, a low-warranty-claim record, and ongoing GAF testing ([goingwithgibbs.com](https://goingwithgibbs.com/gaf-vs-certainteed-vs-owens-corning-which-manufacturer-certification-matters-most/)). OC Platinum Preferred is the scarcest — **invitation-only**, highest satisfaction scores of any OC tier, well under 10% (often cited <1%) ([owenscorning.com](https://www.owenscorning.com/en-us/roofing/contractors/locations)). CertainTeed SELECT ShingleMaster is craft-gated (~top 1%; 50% of crew + all supervisors credentialed) ([certainteed.com](https://www.certainteed.com/select-shinglemaster)). For an uncertified roofer this pillar is a "get certified, then claim it" roadmap — not an immediate deliverable.

**One-time vs ongoing:** ONE-TIME. The action is completing the profile and pasting in the website link — independent guidance literally says "ensure your profile is complete and consists of a link to your site," and notes many contractors leave it unclaimed ([roofingwebmasters.com](https://www.roofingwebmasters.com/link-building/)). In scope for KM.

**Approximate one-time cost:** the link itself is $0 marginal — only the labor to complete/verify the profile (the cert dues are a separate, pre-existing business cost the contractor already pays to be certified). No recurring link spend.

**Impact vs effort:** LOW effort, MODERATE-but-FOUNDATIONAL impact. One credential link won't move rankings alone — it's a high-trust base link in a diversified profile plus a referral/E-E-A-T signal. Promote it as the high-floor, low-effort foundation of the link profile, not the lever that wins organic dominance by itself.

**What NOT to oversell:** it's one credential-gated dofollow link — a high-floor foundation + referral channel, not a ranking silver bullet, not a quotable DA/DR score, and CertainTeed/association equity is unverified until the specific profile is inspected.

**Pillar guardrails — what NOT to oversell:**

### Lever pillar L4 — On-site local-proof layer (schema/entity)
## PILLAR L4 — On-Site Local-Proof Layer (What We Build)

**Bottom line: the on-site layer does not win the map pack. It makes you eligible and it drives organic location-query visibility.** Sell it on what it actually does, not on rank.

Google attributes the pack's link and review signals to off-site sources, not to your markup ([support.google.com](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en)). In the 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors survey, on-page/website signals carry roughly 15% of local-pack weight — third lever, behind Google Business Profile (~32%) and reviews (~20%) ([brightlocal.com](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/google-local-algorithm-and-ranking-factors/)). So the schema/NAP/map layer is the third lever for the pack and a real organic lever — never the pack-ranking driver.

**Schema is entity hygiene, not a ranking boost.** Google's John Mueller is on record that structured data is not a direct ranking factor — "it doesn't mean that we would show it to more users or that it would rank better" ([searchenginejournal.com](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ranking-factors/schema-markup-ranking/)). `sameAs` declares your business is the same entity as records at external URLs, which is how Google reconciles you into the Knowledge Graph — entity clarity, not a multiplier ([willscott.me](https://willscott.me/2025/07/30/sameas-versus-knowsabout-in-schema/)). A connected `@graph` keyed by `@id` (LocalBusiness, location nodes, services, breadcrumbs referencing each other) is the correct structure for machine reconciliation, and `LocalBusiness` carries `geo`, `areaServed`, and `PostalAddress` to declare served geography from one config block ([schema.org](https://schema.org/LocalBusiness)). The cause→effect: lock the entity, become legible and eligible, then organic depth moves position.

**What the entity layer buys is eligibility, which precedes ranking.** Entity resolution runs before content retrieval in both classic and AI retrieval — a brand that fails recognition is excluded before scoring begins ([organikpi.com](https://organikpi.com/blog/technical-seo/schema-sameas-entity-disambiguation-ai-citations/), vendor-incentive, modeled). That reframes the layer as table-stakes to be in the consideration set, especially as AI-cited URLs increasingly fall outside the organic top-10.

**The measured organic payoff is location-query visibility, not pack position.** An independent 85-day controlled test (11 test / 4 control pages) using `spatialCoverage`/`audience` plus `sameAs` to link locations to Wikipedia/Wikidata/Google KG produced +46% impressions and +42% clicks on non-branded location queries ([schemaapp.com](https://www.schemaapp.com/schema-markup/measurable-impact-of-scaling-entity-linking-for-entity-disambiguation/), vendor-incentive, measured). Floor: a single small vendor experiment on organic impressions/clicks. Ceiling: directional evidence that config-driven geo entity-linking moves location-query visibility. It is not a pack-ranking or lead-volume number — never relabel it as one.

**The GBP landing-page target is a config choice, not a default.** Sterling Sky's controlled test confirms the URL you link from the GBP impacts pack ranking for that page's queries ([sterlingsky.ca](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/does-the-url-you-link-to-in-google-my-business-impact-ranking-in-the-local-pack/)). But their own default for a single-location business is the homepage (most authority), and post-2025 Diversity Update, pointing the GBP at your already-top-ranking page can hurt. So: single-location flagship → homepage; multi-location / practitioner / non-ranking page → a dedicated NAP-matched city/service page that lists all services to trigger "website mentions" justifications ([sterlingsky.ca](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/google-business-profile-landing-pages/)). Either way the page must be deep — Sterling Sky's 8,186-business / 200-city study found a slight positive correlation between more substantive words on the linked page and better local rankings, and a negative correlation for hiding the address ([sterlingsky.ca](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/what-gets-you-ranking-for-near-me-2025/)). This is the anti-doorway build rule: deep real content per location beats thin city pages.

**NAP: claim the mechanism, not a number.** A single config-driven NAP source-of-truth rendered identically in schema, footer, and contact page — matching the GBP and every citation — is the build spec. Consistency matters more than volume; inconsistency confuses entity resolution ([brightlocal.com](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/what-is-nap/), vendor-incentive). The circulating "40% lift / 156% Maps visibility / 27% drop" figures trace to no primary study — do not put a NAP percentage in the reel.

**Map embeds are trust/UX, not ranking.** Embed one real map on the contact/location page for NAP confirmation, lazy-loaded for Core Web Vitals (page speed is a ranking factor). Do not spin up hundreds of map-embed geo pages — Google cracked down on that in 2018 ([gobluemedia.com](https://www.gobluemedia.com/blog/google-maps-seo-rankings/)).

### Lever economics
- **One-time vs ongoing:** This pillar is **100% one-time, shippable** — `@graph`/`sameAs`/`LocalBusiness` schema, the NAP source-of-truth, the lazy-loaded map, and the deep city/service landing pages all ship at build and persist with zero monthly labor. Ongoing client-managed off-page (citations upkeep, review velocity) is out of scope here.
- **Equity vs citation / dofollow vs nofollow:** Not applicable as a backlink lever — the on-site layer creates **no external link equity**. `sameAs` is an entity-reconciliation declaration (citation-class identity signal), not a dofollow backlink. Treat it as entity plumbing, not link building. (No PBN, no link schemes, no gray-hat — excluded by design.)
- **Approximate one-time cost:** Low. It is config + template work driven off one GEOGRAPHY/BUSINESS block — schema and NAP are near-free once the template carries them; the cost is writing genuinely deep per-location content (the anti-doorway tax), not the markup.
- **Impact vs effort:** **Low effort, foundational impact — but bounded.** High leverage as the eligibility floor (you become legible to pack, organic, and AI retrieval) and a modest, measured organic-visibility lever. Low leverage as a direct pack-rank mover (~15% of pack weight, and Google credits the link/review portion off-site). Build it because it is cheap, one-time, and table-stakes — not because it ranks you.

### What NOT to oversell

- Never hard-code "dedicated city page per GBP" as the default — single-location flagships link the homepage.

**Pillar guardrails — what NOT to oversell:**

### Lever pillar L5 — The review-acquisition machine
## PILLAR L5 — The Review-Acquisition Machine

**Bottom line.** Reviews are the fastest controllable local-search lever, and the lever is VELOCITY — fresh reviews per month — not raw total. King Maker ships a compliant, single-path review funnel plus the physical artifacts (Google QR + place-ID link on trucks, invoices, door-hangers); the client runs the actual send. The mechanism's job is to clear the early review floor fast and then sustain a steady monthly drip into the organic-prominence and trust engine. It is a necessary, high-correlation input — not a deterministic ranking dial.

**The spine: velocity, not volume.** Review recency moved to a top-5 local ranking factor in 2025, up from #20 in 2023 ([whitespark.ca](https://whitespark.ca/blog/the-most-underrated-local-ranking-factor-in-2025/)); a business earning ~20 fresh reviews/month outranks one sitting on 200 reviews from two years ago ([whitespark.ca](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/)). Rankings "fall off a cliff" when collection stops — an 18-day pause did it in Sterling Sky's 8,186-business / 200-city study, and recovery follows resumption ([sterlingsky.ca](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/number-of-reviews-impact-ranking/)). That is the whole argument for a machine that produces a continuous drip rather than a one-time backfill. The early floor matters too: crossing 9→10 reviews produced a small ranking bump in all 3 test cases, with diminishing returns after ([sterlingsky.ca](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/number-of-reviews-impact-ranking/)) — so clear the low roofing bar quickly, then let recency carry the weight.

**Compliance is a build constraint, not a config option.** Review GATING (happy customers get the Google link, unhappy ones get a private form) violates Google's UGC policy: businesses must not selectively solicit positive reviews or discourage negative ones ([support.google.com](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en)). It is also federal-law exposure — the FTC's final Rule on Consumer Reviews (effective Oct 21, 2024) bans review suppression and sentiment-incentivized reviews, with civil penalties up to ~$51,744 per violation ([alston.com](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2024/10/ftc-issues-final-rule-on-fake-reviews-testimonials)). Many off-the-shelf "review funnels" sold to contractors ARE gating tools, so the KM funnel must be single-path and unscreened BY DESIGN — every customer gets the same Google link, no incentive. Genuine, unincentivized solicitation is explicitly permitted ([support.google.com](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en)); the machine operates inside that green light. Route the /review path to Google's own first-party shareable link + QR code, not a third-party intermediary ([support.google.com](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815?hl=en)).

**Site-side reviews are content, not stars.** Self-hosted reviews on the reviewed entity's own site are INELIGIBLE for Google star rich-results under LocalBusiness or any Organization type — whether marked up directly or via an embedded Google/Facebook widget ([developers.google.com](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet)); BrightLocal corroborates and points to Product/Service-scoped schema as the eligible path ([brightlocal.com](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/review-schema/)). So a whole-company site-side aggregateRating will NOT render stars in search. Ship it as on-page content, E-E-A-T, and an AI-answer/entity signal — and reserve "star results" talk for Product/Service schema where genuinely applicable. Selling site reviews as "Google stars" is both ineffective and an FTC deceptive-claim risk.

**The request flow (client-run).** SMS out-opens email and converts better; fire within 24–48h of job completion, two sentences plus the link, personalized, with a single reminder at 3–5 days (more damages the relationship) ([wiserreview.com](https://wiserreview.com/blog/review-request-message/)). Point-of-job physical asks (QR/NFC cards, truck and invoice decals) lift volume at peak satisfaction. The conversion multipliers — ~98% SMS open vs ~20–30% email, 12–20% vs 3–8% completion, "3–5x" from cards — come from review-platform vendors who profit from the claim and rarely publish methodology ([birdeye.com](https://birdeye.com/blog/sms-vs-email-review-requests-2025/), [wiserreview.com](https://wiserreview.com/blog/google-review-card/)). Promise the floor (SMS reliably out-opens email; point-of-job physical asks raise volume); label the headline multipliers vendor-reported/directional.

**Scope, equity, cost, impact.** ONE-TIME / shippable: the single-path funnel build, the /review page wired to Google's place-ID link, the QR/NFC artifact set, and Product/Service review schema where applicable. ONGOING send cadence is the client's to run — out of scope here. Equity: reviews are a CITATION/trust/entity signal, not a backlink — there is no dofollow vs nofollow link-equity dimension to this lever (no PBNs, no link schemes, none of it touches this pillar). Approximate one-time cost: low — funnel + schema + artifact design is a small build line, the cost sits in the client's ongoing labor to ask. Impact-vs-effort: HIGH impact / LOW one-time effort — it is the cheapest, fastest-moving controllable signal, which is exactly why it anchors the offer.

**What NOT to oversell:** reviews are ~16–20% of pack weight while proximity (~55%) dominates ([sterlingsky.ca](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/number-of-reviews-impact-ranking/)) — this lever moves a heavily-correlated signal toward organic prominence and conversion; it does not buy a top-3 pack spot, and the pack stays a proximity-capped byproduct we never headline.

**Pillar guardrails — what NOT to oversell:**
- Do NOT sell reviews as a deterministic ranking dial — reviews are ~16-20% of pack weight while proximity (~55%) dominates; the lever is heavily correlated and consensus-backed, not proven-causal (Sterling Sky/SEL caution correlation; one plausible mechanism is indirect via engagement/CTR).
- Do NOT promise a top-3 map-pack spot from reviews — the pack is proximity-capped; center the machine on organic regional dominance and conversion/trust where reviews carry full weight.
- Do NOT assert the SMS/card multipliers (98% open, 12-20% completion, 3-5x volume) as measured — they are vendor-reported/directional; promise only the floor (SMS out-opens email; point-of-job physical asks raise volume).
- Do NOT claim site-side aggregateRating produces Google star results — self-hosted LocalBusiness/Organization reviews are ineligible; reserve star-results talk for Product/Service schema where genuinely applicable.
- Do NOT ship or imply a gated 'happy/unhappy fork' funnel — it violates Google policy and the FTC rule; the funnel is single-path and unscreened by design.
- Do NOT promise an ever-climbing review TOTAL as the value — the measurable bump is the early floor (9->10) plus sustained recency; sell consistency/velocity, not a vanity count.
- Do NOT frame reviews as a backlink/equity play — there is no dofollow/nofollow dimension; it is a citation/trust/entity signal, and no PBN or link-scheme tactics touch this lever.

Frame this as an asset + outreach machinery with an *earned* outcome — floor is the asset shipping, not a placement.

But several top independent experts call citations "almost a non-factor" today, and mass citation-building is counterproductive — ~50 at once produced no pack lift and duplicate listings get deindexed ([ricketyroo.com/blog/do-citations-still-matter-local-seo](https://ricketyroo.com/blog/do-citations-still-matter-local-seo/)). Position the citation set as clean, consistent, indexed NAP hygiene — a quality core, never a count of hundreds.

# Part E — Honesty & defensibility guardrails (pre-publish checklist)

**Never claim**

- "Your pages/site will win the map pack" — the pack is GBP+reviews+proximity-weighted (GBP ~32% / reviews ~20% / on-page only ~15%; brightlocal.com/learn/google-local-algorithm-and-ranking-factors). Pages drive ORGANIC, not the pack. The site only tiebreaks the pack when GBP + review velocity are already equal.
- A precise proximity percentage stated as settled Google fact — e.g. "proximity is 55% of pack ranking." The 55.2% figure is one measured XGBoost study (pos 1-21; drops to 36.2% in top 10, varies law 69.4% / food ~30%) accessed via a secondary write-up; cite it as a single modeled/measured study with its range and vertical-variance, never as a universal constant.
- A point-promise leads/jobs/week or guaranteed-traffic number — e.g. "you'll get 15 roofing jobs a month." Organic conversion is not a higher raw rate than paid (organic 4.9% vs paid 5.4%; ruleranalytics.com), the edge is intent-mix/ticket/owned-environment, and ~90% of pages get zero organic traffic, so any volume number is a point-promise we can't defend.
- "Guaranteed rankings" or "guaranteed top-3 / page-one" — organic compounds over weeks-to-18mo and is never guaranteed (only ~1.74% of new pages reach top 10 within a year; ahrefs.com). Tie the guarantee to a verifiable ranking RANGE/floor, not a certainty.
- PBNs, private blog networks, link farms, bought/sold links, excessive link exchanges, automated link creation, or any gray-hat link scheme — Google's spam policy names all of these as violations (developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies); thin-site over-optimization with optimized anchor text most often costs the GAIN via SpamBrain devaluation, with demotion as the tail risk. Excluded explicitly and permanently.
- Yext (or any rental listings service) sold as an OWNED asset — Yext listings degrade on cancellation (~60% disappeared/incorrect in an n=60 study). Only Whitespark-style one-time/ownership-transferred citation builds are "owned" (whitespark.ca/listings-service).
- Self-hosted reviews shown as Google star rich-results — reviews on the reviewed entity's own site under LocalBusiness/Organization schema are ineligible for star rich-results (developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet). Ship site-side reviews as content / E-E-A-T / AI-entity signal, never "star results."
- "SOC 2 / bank-level / HIPAA / compliant / certified" security or any equivalent attestation for the website — position only on OWNED ACTIONS (OWASP-hardened, zero-stored-data). The FTC enforces false security claims independent of any breach.
- "Schema/structured data ranks you higher" — schema is not a direct ranking factor (Mueller: structured data "doesn't mean… it would rank better"; searchenginejournal.com/ranking-factors/schema-markup-ranking). Its value is eligibility + machine-legibility + AI-citation, not rank.
- "NAP consistency makes you 40% more likely to appear in the local pack" — this widely-circulated stat traces to NO primary BrightLocal source and must be excluded from reels/pitches (verified unverifiable; brightlocal.com/learn/what-is-nap).
- "Citations boost your rankings" framed as a growth lever, or NoFollow citations sold as backlinks — citation weight is low and declining (~6% pack / 7% organic, down from 13% in 2020) and most tier-1 directories are NoFollow (BBB/Foursquare/Houzz/Angi/Yelp). Sell citations as foundational hygiene + AI-visibility (~13%) + SERP-interception, not link equity. Never call Yelp DoFollow — its links are redirected + nofollow.

- A precise GBP suspension annual base rate — only state the directional, sourced facts (suspension reports up 80%+ since 2023; appeal waits ~5-6 weeks early 2025; sterlingsky.ca) as the "rented pin vs owned domain" risk argument.
