# Off-market scouting notes — Sean

Generated 2026-08-05 by the land campaign.

## Area note 1
Wake's tax-foreclosure pipeline runs through the Sheriff's Office at the county courthouse after Tax Administration dockets a judgment (roughly 4 months from delinquency to sale), with a mandatory 10-day upset-bid window afterward (minimum raise 5% or $750) — the 4 real cases live on wake.gov as of this session (all Raleigh, sale dates still "To Be Announced") were improved in-town parcels, suggesting Wake's active foreclosure pipeline skews toward houses rather than raw land, so a scout should not expect much vacant-lot volume from that specific channel. A more promising GIS-driven approach is screening Wake's iMAPS/parcels open-data layer for large parcels carrying the PUV present-use-value deferred-tax code (NCGS 105-277.2, agricultural/forestry/horticultural) with near-zero building value — these are unimproved tracts whose owners get a tax cliff (rollback taxes) if they ever subdivide or sell for development, which is exactly the pressure point that produces off-market deals, concentrated in the fast-growing eastern-Wake corridor (Zebulon/Wendell/Knightdale) where several listings harvested this session sit right on the Wake/Franklin/Johnston county line. Private land-auction houses (Iron Horse, Tranzon, Rogers) run Wake parcels only sporadically — none had a live Wake land auction scheduled this session, the only concrete Wake hit found was from 2015 — so auction cadence here is better tracked by periodically re-checking those three sites than relied on as a steady inbound channel.

## Area note 2
Johnston County's own GIS/tax portal (mapclick8.johnstonnc.com, tax search at johnstonnc.gov/tax2) supports owner-mailing-address and PUV (present-use-value/ag-deferral) lookups, making it feasible to hand-build a vacant-parcel-plus-absentee-owner scouting list by pulling large PUV-coded tracts near the Clayton/Four Oaks/Archer Lodge growth corridor and cross-referencing them against the county's septic/well permit-status search at johnstonnc.gov/qpsa/envsearch - an online tool most rural NC counties don't expose, and one that lets a scout pre-screen buildability before ever contacting an owner. Auction/disposition cadence is effectively zero right now: the county's own foreclosure page shows "No Sale Scheduled," and the one active local auction house (Johnson Properties, Angier) is not keeping its Johnston listings current - both candidates checked this session had already sold - so tax-sale and forced-auction channels are not a productive scouting lane for this county at present. The higher-yield off-market play is large PUV/ag-deferred tracts (roughly 15-40 acres) held by long-tenure owners along the I-540-adjacent Clayton/Archer Lodge growth edge, since a deferred-tax parcel approaching a rollback trigger (ownership transfer, subdivision pressure, deferral-period expiry) is the classic pre-listing signal in a fast-growing county like this one.

## Area note 3
Harnett County's clearest off-market signal is its courthouse tax-foreclosure calendar: the county runs periodic sales under NC's standard process (10-day upset-bid window, 5%-or-$750 minimum raise) — a real one is confirmed for 2026-08-28 covering parcels in Dunn, Cameron, Spring Lake, and Broadway, but minimum bids and acreage post as individual PDF notices per parcel rather than in a scrapeable list, so a human scout needs to open each notice directly at harnett.org/tax/. Johnson Properties Realtors & Auctioneers, based in Angier, runs a frequent (roughly biweekly Wednesday) live/online auction cadence that regularly includes subdivided Harnett land — one recent example split a Coats/Dunn farm into 10 tracts from 0.59 to 11.82 acres — and the firm often dual-lists the same parcel simultaneously in the MLS, so a scouting workflow should check their site directly (johnsonproperties.com/auctions) rather than rely on aggregators, which were seen carrying at least one of their auctions as "active" a full four months after it actually closed. For GIS-driven vacant-parcel scouting, the county exposes a real, publicly browsable ArcGIS REST catalog (gis.harnett.org/arcgis/rest/services) with a dedicated Tax parcels service plus Planning/Addressing/Broadband layers — a present-use-value (PUV/ag-deferment) or minimal-improvement-value query against that layer is the standard way to find land quietly likely to hit the market, though the public map viewer itself doesn't expose bulk query/export. The Sheriff's Office execution-sale channel is real but currently dormant (explicitly "0 Auction... scheduled") since it runs ad hoc off civil judgments rather than a fixed calendar, so it's worth a periodic re-check rather than a one-time pull.

## Area note 4
Franklin County is running no standing auction or county-surplus inventory as of this pull — Iron Horse, Auction.com, and the county's own surplus-property page all came back confirmed-empty on direct fetch, so auction activity here is opportunistic/event-driven rather than a steady channel; a scouting list should poll these periodically rather than expect continuous supply. Tax-foreclosure parcels do move through the standard 10-day upset-bid cycle (NCGS 1-339.25, deposit = 5% of bid or $750 whichever is greater), but current case-level notices live at the Clerk of Superior Court's filings, not a self-serve county web page, so a scout would need to check courthouse postings or a legal-notice service directly rather than a URL. For GIS-based off-market sourcing, Franklin County's own parcel/tax-value layers (franklincountymaps.net for GIS, franklincountytax.us for owner/value search) support querying by improvement-value=0 plus long-tenure owner-mailing-address-mismatch to build a vacant-land direct-mail list — a classic pattern for absentee-owned rural tracts. Several small unincorporated pockets (the Alert community off GF Ball Rd/Alert Rd, the Reed Road corridor near Castalia, and Stallings Mill Rd east of Louisburg) show family-held tracts being incrementally subdivided into 5-15-acre pieces and marketed first through boutique land brokerages (Carolina Forestry & Realty, Whitetail Properties) rather than hitting the big portals immediately — tracking those two brokerages' own new-listing pages directly is a leading indicator ahead of MLS syndication, and is worth a recurring manual check for a human scout.

## Area note 5
Durham County/City run a joint ArcGIS parcel system (maps.durhamnc.gov, webgis.durhamnc.gov/server/rest/services, and the live-durhamnc.opendata.arcgis.com parcels layer) that exposes owner mailing addresses and Present-Use-Value (PUV) tags; PUV-deferred parcels in the rural NW county (Bahama/Rougemont) are typically multi-generational farm or timber holdings and are the highest-signal off-market target for a mail or knock campaign, since owners already show as long-hold and undeveloped. Durham County tax foreclosure auctions run monthly (2nd Tuesday, noon, Durham County Justice Center, 510 S Dillard St, per NCGS 105-374 with a 10-day upset-bid window), but the public docket was empty at this pull and the county's own site disclaims it "cannot be relied upon to be up-to-date in real time" - cadence is real but lumpy, so a recurring monthly re-check (or a direct call to sale Commissioner Doug Davis) beats a one-time scrape. Both the City of Durham and Durham County surplus-property channels confirmed they carry no standing scrapeable list - City contact is the Real Estate Division (2011 Fay St, 919-560-4197), County contact is the Open Space & Real Estate Division (919-560-0079) - so off-market surplus deals there depend on getting on their notification list rather than a repeatable web check.

## Area note 6
Orange County's tax office runs In Rem foreclosures under NC G.S. 105-375 with the standard 10-day upset-bid window (5%+ or $750 minimum raise over the high bid); nothing is in that pipeline today (confirmed live via orangecountync.gov/905), but the site's own Notice-of-Sale-by-Upset-Bid archive shows the county does cycle small (~1-1.5 acre) parcels through periodically, so a monthly recheck of orangecountync.gov/902 (Tax Auctions) is worth automating rather than treating today's zero as permanent. Iron Horse Auction Company runs periodic online-only Orange County land auctions (most recent found: a 31-acre, 2-lot Bama Rd/Hensley Rd tract, March 2026) but keeps no clean archive/upcoming list on-site, so this channel needs a recurring direct check rather than a one-time search. For GIS-based off-market sourcing, Orange County's ArcGIS MapServer (gis.orangecountync.gov/arcgis/rest/services/WebMainService/MapServer) exposes parcel/PIN/owner layers queryable for large vacant parcels with no recent deed activity or absentee-owner mailing addresses — classic vacant-land direct-mail sourcing — though the GIS web app itself posted a maintenance window for Aug 5-6, 2026 (today/tomorrow per the census note), so live querying should wait a day or two. Separately, the market itself skews expensive: nearly every verified listing this session priced at $600K+ (Research-Triangle/UNC-adjacent land), so off-market/GIS-sourced parcels are likely the more realistic route to sub-$300K inventory than the standing-listing channels harvested here.

## Area note 7
Chatham County publishes a live ArcGIS REST cadastral layer (gisservices.chathamcountync.gov) that can be queried directly for parcels carrying a Present-Use-Value ag/forestry tax deferral — a classic off-market signal, since owners paying reduced tax on raw acreage are prime cold-outreach targets, especially along the fast-appreciating Siler City/US-64/Moncure corridor where the Wolfspeed plant and the adjacent 1,326-acre Carolina Core Parkway megasite have pushed land values up sharply in the surrounding area. There is no standing local auction house for the county — inventory instead comes from statewide players working the area on a rolling basis (Iron Horse Auction Company closed a 68-acre Goldston timber/creek-frontage tract online on 2026-07-21; Rogers Realty & Auction also covers the county through its Triad-MLS property search), so a scouting list should monitor those catalogs periodically rather than expect a fixed local sale calendar. Tax-foreclosure and upset-bid sales are handled directly by the Chatham County Tax/Finance office rather than through a self-serve online portal (the county's own listing page blocked automated access this pass), so that channel needs a direct call or visit to the tax office/register of deeds rather than remote monitoring.

## Area note 8
Granville's Oxford/Creedmoor/Stem growth corridor (NC-96, US-15/56, Bob Daniel Rd, Wilson Town Rd, and the Jonathans Landing/Kerr Lake pocket near Bullock) shows a clear pattern of small local land-subdividers cutting 1-12 acre wooded tracts off larger farms and drip-feeding them onto the market a few at a time through a handful of repeat listing agents (Julie Wright Land Company; Nathan Watson/Northern Point Realty). Cold-contacting those agents directly for unlisted "next phase" tracts on the same roads (Bob Daniel, Brassfield, Wolf Pit, Runner Stone) is likely the highest-yield off-market lead in this county. No active land auctions were found this pass at Auction.com, Iron Horse Auction, or Rogers Realty & Auction; Iron Horse (Rockingham, NC) is the regional consignment house most likely to carry a future NC rural-land auction and is worth a recurring check, though none of the three houses focus on Granville specifically. The county's own tax-search portal (tax.granvillecounty.org) is parcel-lookup only, not a foreclosure feed — genuine tax-foreclosure/upset-bid inventory would need a Register of Deeds / Clerk of Court notice check rather than a web scrape. For GIS-sourced vacant-parcel prospecting, the county's ArcGIS parcel viewer (granvillecounty.maps.arcgis.com, webappviewer id 537f4daef87e42538ffc4d6de338a7a1) supports querying by low/zero improvement value against large-acreage parcels — almost certainly how the repeat subdividers above are sourcing their raw tracts before cutting them into the small on-market lots seen this pass.

## Area note 9
Off-market inventory in Nash/Wilson skews toward large-acreage farm and timber tracts moving through local land brokerages (Land and Farms Realty, Mossy Oak, Stone Auction & Realty) rather than the national portals; several parcels harvested here are explicitly "to be divided out of" a larger farm (e.g. the Battleboro 20ac carved from a 600ac farm, the Wilson Ward St. 17ac with a 71-unit concept plan), suggesting local owners parcel off buildable lots opportunistically rather than through formally platted subdivisions - a pattern a human scout could exploit by asking these same brokers about unlisted "back of the farm" inventory. Nash County's GIS parcel viewer (gis.nashcountync.gov) and Wilson's (gis.wilson-co.com / wilsoncounty.maps.arcgis.com) both block generic bot fetches but work fine in a real browser - a scout can query vacant/unimproved-value parcels there directly, then cross-reference owner mailing addresses via the county tax-search sites (Nash: taxdata.nashcountync.gov; Wilson: wilsonnc.devnetwedge.com) to cold-contact owners of never-listed vacant tracts. Auction cadence is currently thin: Iron Horse, Tranzon, and Auction.com all show zero live NC-land auctions in this geography as of Aug 2026, so upset-bid tax-foreclosure notices (posted individually by the Nash/Wilson Clerk of Court and county tax offices, not aggregated on a searchable web portal) are the more promising off-market lead - worth a periodic phone check rather than a web crawl.

## Area note 11
Both counties run public parcel viewers that support DIY off-market prospecting: Lee County's ArcGIS webapp + tax-access GIS (taxaccess.leecountync.gov) and Moore County's ArcGIS Experience Builder + iCare/iasWorld tax lookup (icare.moorecountync.gov) both let you filter by land-use code, zero/low building value, and long ownership tenure to build a vacant-parcel candidate list -- that filter (vacant-classified parcel + no recent sale + absentee mailing address) is the standard proxy for an off-market lead in this market. On-the-ground auction activity is real but thin and irregular: Iron Horse Auction/Properties (regionally based, confirmed active in Moore County via a standing Carthage brokerage listing) runs both conventional land sales and online-only land auctions across the Sandhills on a rolling multi-week cadence, while Rogers Realty & Auction Co. runs county-surplus, municipal-vehicle, and estate auctions region-wide, though nothing NC-Sandhills-specific was live in either firm's calendar this pass. Neither Lee nor Moore County publishes a clean self-service tax-foreclosure/upset-bid listing page (both guessed URLs 404'd or bounced to a generic department-contact page), so upset-bid tracking in practice likely means watching legal notices in the Sanford Herald / The Pilot (Southern Pines) or calling the tax collector's office directly. No septic-permit search portal is publicly live for either county (Lee: fee schedule only, no database; Moore: applications go by email to Environmental Health), so septic feasibility on raw parcels stays a manual county call rather than a scrapeable data point.

## Area note 13
Hoke County runs a standing tax-foreclosure/upset-bid "Surplus Properties for Sale" list published directly by the Clerk to the Board (910-875-8751, hokecounty.net/474) with parcel IDs, minimum bids, and rolling 10-day upset-bid windows - it turns over via serial re-bids, so a monthly recheck is the highest-yield off-market move for that county specifically. Cumberland County does NOT self-publish an equivalent web list; its tax-foreclosure/upset-bid sales are handled directly through the Tax Collector/Register of Deeds offices (not a self-service page), with third-party trackers (northcarolinataxdeeds.com, surplusfundslist.com) indexing the county at a summary level only - a phone/in-person check-in is required to see live parcels. Neither county currently has any active inventory with the major land-auction houses (Iron Horse, Rogers, Tranzon, Auction.com all returned zero live Cumberland/Hoke land listings this pass), so auction cadence here runs on occasional multi-tract estate/farm sales rather than a steady drumbeat - GIS vacant-parcel scans (Cumberland's ArcGIS MapServer at gis.co.cumberland.nc.us, Hoke's maps.hokecounty.org) cross-referenced against the tax-assessor owner-mailing-address field (out-of-state or corporate-LLC owners on long-held vacant parcels) would be the more productive manual-scouting technique than watching the auction houses.

## Area note 14

For GIS-based vacant-parcel scouting, Alamance's ArcGIS hub (apps.alamance-nc.com/GISMap, plus the county's open-data portal at alamancecounty-alamancectygis.opendata.arcgis.com) supports querying by land-use/improvement-value codes to surface unlisted buildable parcels, and notably the county's UDO confirms unincorporated Alamance carries no traditional zoning at all — a materially important fact for any buildable-lot feasibility check there. Recurring hunting-land brokerages (Mossy Oak Properties in particular) refresh Caswell's larger-acreage (8-40ac) rural tracts every few weeks and were a more productive auction-adjacent channel this pass than any of the formal auction houses — Iron Horse, Rogers Realty, and Tranzon all came up empty for active Alamance/Caswell land lots at the time of this run.
