  **5. NC-specific traps the plan doesn't know it doesn't know** (this is the required-coverage checklist for K1/K2, every item citation-mandated):
  - *"Perc" is a colloquialism.* NC septic approval = soil evaluation → Improvement Permit (IP) → Authorization to Construct. Modern IPs tied to a documented site are generally non-expiring if nothing changes; older ones died at 5 years. A listing saying "perked in 2019" may mean a valid IP, an expired one, or a realtor's optimism. The IP states *bedroom count and system type* — that number, not the acreage, bounds the house.
  - *County health-department reality*: Wake and Johnston Environmental Health have run multi-week backlogs for septic/well permits; NC offers faster private paths (licensed soil scientists; the statutory "engineered option" permit route). Cost/timeline claims must be per-county and current, not statewide folklore.
  - *Riparian buffers*: Neuse Basin and Jordan Lake nutrient rules impose protected buffers (commonly 50 ft) on intermittent/perennial streams across most of Wake/Durham/Johnston/Chatham — a "buildable 1-acre lot" with a blue-line stream loses both banks' buffer from its usable area. The plan says "wetlands" generically and misses the NC-specific buffer regime entirely.
  - *FEMA buyout parcels*: post-Matthew/Florence buyouts in Cumberland/Fayetteville and along the Neuse created deed-restricted *open-space* parcels that can never legally be built on — and they surface in feeds as suspiciously cheap "vacant lots." This needs an explicit kill rule in P3, not a knowledge-layer footnote.
  - *Present-Use Value rollback*: farm/forestland under PUV carries deferred taxes; disqualification (e.g., your customer builds) triggers 3 years of back taxes plus interest. Cheap Granville/Franklin/Harnett acreage is frequently PUV — a hidden cost field the schema lacks.
  - *Heirs property*: fractionated family ownership is endemic in the rural counties; NC adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act (2020), which changes partition sales but does nothing to make a 14-heir parcel quick to buy — K3's off-market outreach will hit this wall constantly and must warn: title clearance takes months and an attorney.
  - *NCDOT driveway permits*: NC maintains most roads (2nd-largest state system); any new connection to a state road needs an NCDOT driveway permit with sight-distance standards; frontage on a major corridor (US-70, NC-42, NC-50) can be denied or conditioned on turn-lane improvements costing five figures. "Road frontage: yes" is not "you may build a driveway."
  - *No family-subdivision exemption*: G.S. 160D-802's exemptions are recombination, >10-acre divisions without ROW dedication, public strips, and the ≤2-acre-into-≤3-lots split — there is no family-transfer exemption, a myth rural landowners believe; the knowledge layer should bust it.
  - *Well-yield risk zones*: Triassic-basin (Deep River/Dan River) fractured-bedrock areas in parts of Wake/Chatham/Lee/Durham have notoriously variable well yield and quality; "well needed" is a $6–15k gamble, not a line item.
  - *Water/sewer capacity and tap fees*: growth-pressure towns (Johnston County sewer allocation, Fuquay-Varina/Holly Springs tap-fee escalation) plus private utilities (e.g., Aqua NC) with high rates in rural subdivisions — "water at road" can still mean a $3–8k tap and a meter wait.
  - *Transaction mechanics*: NC is an attorney-closing, due-diligence-fee state (the DD fee is non-refundable and paid to the seller), vacant-land purchases use the vacant-lot contract form (12-T), and the residential property disclosure statement does not apply to unimproved land — caveat emptor is literal here; mineral/timber rights severance is a live title issue in the eastern counties.
