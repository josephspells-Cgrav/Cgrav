# Dossier 01 — Above-the-Fold, Hero-as-Transaction & Proof

**Project:** Summit & Oak Roofing (DEMO) — Raleigh NC + Cary/Knightdale/Garner/Clayton/Apex
**Traffic model:** cold ORGANIC search, high-intent homeowners who do NOT know the brand
**Default lane:** storm/emergency-led
**Conversion target:** 8–14% per-page (top-quartile→top-decile for contractor pages)
**Date:** 2026-06-17

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The above-the-fold (ATF) region is the single highest-leverage real estate on the page, and the evidence is unambiguous that it still governs conversion in 2026. Eye-tracking shows elements above the fold are viewed ~102% more than elements just below it, and it is normal for fewer than 50% of users to scroll past the fold at all (NN/g). For a cold-organic, storm-led roofing page, that means **every decision input a homeowner needs must be resolvable without scrolling** — or it effectively does not exist for half the audience.

The biggest, most repeatable wins from the 2024–2026 CRO data:

1. **Hero must be a transaction surface, not a poster.** Putting the lead capture *in* the hero (vs. a CTA that jumps to a form below) produced an immediate +15% in a documented home-services test (ClickMechanic). The hero's job is to let a homeowner act in the first viewport.

2. **A multi-step quiz beats a flat single form for roofing** — by large margins in the data (single-step 4.53% → multi-step 13.85%, a +206% lift in one analysis; Venture Harbour found +86% vs. single-page; mobile multi-step +30–60% per the contractor-specific source). The mechanism is Cialdini commitment-consistency: open with a near-zero-risk question (project type / ZIP), defer the contact-gate (name + phone) to the LAST step.

3. **Phone is the highest-converting channel in home services** — phone leads convert at ~46% vs. a ~7.8% all-channel average (nearly 6×). Click-to-call must be prominent ATF on both desktop and mobile, and a **sticky mobile call bar lifts call volume 25–40%.**

4. **Proof density lifts conversion when it is specific.** Reviews/badges + CTA in one layout can "double calls/conversions." Specific embedded reviews (name, neighborhood, date) lift 15–30% vs. generic trust badges. A star-rating + review-count line ("4.9 / 312 reviews") belongs adjacent to the primary CTA, not in a distant testimonials section.

5. **A financing one-liner near the CTA lifts contact rates 12–20%** on replacement pages ("From $X/mo with approved financing") — and financed jobs run ~4.5× larger. This is one of the cheapest ATF wins available and directly serves a decision input.

6. **The 5 decision inputs fit ATF without clutter** by collapsing each to its smallest honest token (a one-line rating, a "from $X/mo" chip, a license # micro-line, a badge row, a single dominant CTA + sticky call) and letting the hero photo + whitespace carry the premium feel. Premium restraint = *one* dominant action, supporting proof as compact glanceable tokens, everything else below the fold.

**Single dominant CTA discipline is non-negotiable:** one primary action produces dramatically more clicks/revenue than competing CTAs (the cited figure is +371% clicks / +1,617% revenue for single-CTA pages). Click-to-call is the natural co-primary because of the 46% phone close rate — treat "call" and "get estimate" as the two faces of one conversion, not four competing buttons.

## Q1 — WHAT BELONGS ABOVE THE FOLD (prioritized element set + order + layout patterns)

### The fold still rules (evidence)
- Elements ATF viewed **~102% more** than just-below-fold elements; people scroll more than before but still look more above the fold (NN/g eye-tracking).
- **<50% of users** typically scroll past the fold; top of page is "red hot" on every heatmap, sharp drop-off right below.
- Real-world: a local service business found **70% of mobile users never saw a mid-page offer**; moving it up **doubled conversions in a month.**
- Mobile users move downward within seconds and engage ATF less per-second than desktop — so on mobile the ATF must be even tighter and the action even more obvious.

### Prioritized ATF element set (cold-organic roofing, storm-led)
Ranked by conversion contribution, synthesized from the contractor-specific sources (Pipeline On, ThreeBird, ResultCalls):

1. **Service + city headline that matches search intent.** Names the problem (storm/emergency roof) AND the metro (Raleigh / Triangle). Cold organic visitors must feel "this is exactly what I searched" in <1s. Use the 4U test (Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific, Useful).
2. **Sub-headline / one-line benefit** — speed + reassurance ("Free inspection, fast response across the Triangle. Licensed & insured.").
3. **Primary action — the transaction.** Either the first step of the in-hero quiz OR a single dominant CTA button. Action-specific verb ("Get My Free Roof Inspection" / "Start My Estimate"), not "Submit."
4. **Click-to-call, visibly co-primary.** Phone number rendered as a tappable `tel:` element + a call button. Phone is the 46%-close channel; it must be a first-class ATF action.
5. **Trust line adjacent to the CTA** — star rating + review count ("4.9 ★ · 312 Google reviews") + license #/years. This is the proof that earns the click; it must sit *with* the action, not 1,000px down.
6. **Financing micro-line** near the CTA — "From $X/mo with approved financing" (12–20% lift; honest "from/example" language for NC compliance).
7. **Real crew/job photo** (NOT stock) as the hero visual — tangible evidence of a real local company; carries the premium aesthetic.
8. **Badge row** (compact) — GAF/manufacturer, BBB, license, Google — small and glanceable, not a wall.

> Order logic: intent-match headline → reassurance → action (form-step or CTA) → proof that de-risks the action → photo/badges as the visual frame. The first three answer "am I in the right place / can they help / what do I do"; the rest answer "why you."

### 2–3 concrete layout patterns from highest-converting examples

**Pattern A — Split hero (form-right / message-left)** — the workhorse for high-intent home services.
- Left column: headline + sub-headline + rating line + financing line + click-to-call + trust badges.
- Right column: the lead capture (multi-step quiz step 1) in a card.
- Background: full-bleed crew/roof photo with a charcoal scrim for contrast.
- Why: keeps message and transaction in the same viewport; eyes land on copy, hand lands on form. This is the dominant pattern in the converting roofing examples.

**Pattern B — Centered hero + form-as-first-action (mobile-first)** — best when mobile is the majority (71% of contractor traffic per Pipeline On).
- Stacked: headline → sub → rating line → the quiz step 1 (single low-risk question, big tap targets) → click-to-call. Photo as background.
- Why: ClickMechanic's in-hero form move = +15% immediately; on mobile the form *is* the hero.

**Pattern C — Hero with dual-action bar + sticky persistence.**
- Headline + sub over photo, then a two-button action row ("Get Free Inspection" primary / "Call (919)…" secondary) with the rating line between them; the call action persists as a sticky bar on scroll (mobile) / sticky header (desktop).
- Why: serves both the form-preferrers and the call-now storm-panic segment without diluting into 4 CTAs.

### BUILD RECOMMENDATIONS — Q1
- **[R1]** Build the hero as a **split layout** (Pattern A) on desktop ≥1024px: 60/40 message/form, full-bleed real-crew photo background + charcoal (#161719) scrim at ~55–65% opacity for AA text contrast.
- **[R2]** Collapse to **Pattern B** under 768px: stack headline → sub → rating line → quiz-step-1 → click-to-call; ensure the quiz step-1 and the call button are both within the first mobile viewport (target the whole stack ≤ ~640px tall).
- **[R3]** Headline must **name the service + metro** and read storm/urgency-first by default (e.g., "Storm-Damaged Roof in Raleigh? Free Inspection, Fast." in Newsreader display). Sub-headline in Plus Jakarta Sans carries reassurance (licensed/insured, service area, speed).
- **[R4]** Place, in this order, within the hero card/column: (1) star+review line, (2) the form/CTA, (3) financing one-liner, (4) badge row — so proof brackets the action.
- **[R5]** Keep ATF to **one dominant visual action** + click-to-call as co-primary. No more than 2 buttons in the hero; never 3–4 competing CTAs.
- **[R6]** Verify with a viewport check at 1366×768 (laptop), 390×844 (iPhone), 768×1024 (tablet) that all 5 decision-input tokens render without scroll.

## Q2 — HERO AS TRANSACTION: form-in-hero vs CTA-to-form vs multi-step quiz

### What the data says
- **Form-in-hero beats CTA-to-form.** Most visitors never scroll past the hero; ClickMechanic moved the quote form *into* the hero → **+15% conversions immediately.** If the form lives below and the hero only has a CTA, you lose the ~50% who don't scroll.
- **Multi-step quiz beats single flat form for this category:**
  - Single-step 4.53% → multi-step **13.85%** (+206%) in one analysis.
  - Venture Harbour: multi-step converts **+86%** vs single-page.
  - Contractor-specific (Pipeline On): multi-step **+30–60% on mobile.**
  - Mobile forms broken into steps: **+63% completion** (Tinyform 2025); +20–30% from cognitive-load reduction generally.
  - Two-step (email → rest) beat single 7-field form **1.8% → 2.9% (+61%).**
- **Why multi-step wins:** Cialdini commitment-consistency — a small first action (project type, ZIP) creates momentum; **81% of users abandon forms after starting**, so partial-capture of the early easy answers matters. Progressive disclosure lowers *perceived* effort even at equal total fields.

### First step & field order (the converting recipe)
- **Open with the lowest-risk, highest-relevance question** — NOT name/phone. Best openers for roofing:
  - "What do you need?" → buttons: *Storm/Leak Repair · Full Replacement · Inspection · Other* (one-tap; routes + qualifies; matches storm-led intent).
  - or "What's your ZIP / project location?" — a "much smaller ask than personal info," and it reinforces the local service-area angle (Raleigh/Cary/etc.).
- **Then context** (1–2 quick taps): roof type / approximate age / "is it actively leaking?" (urgency router).
- **Defer the contact-gate to the LAST step:** name + phone (+ optional email). Phone is the priority field because phone is the 46%-close channel.

### How many fields before the contact-gate
- Keep the **whole flow to 3–4 input "moments"**, and the contact-gate itself to **2–3 fields (name, phone, [email optional]).** Each field beyond ~5 total drops conversion ~20% per field (Pipeline On). Mobile cap: 3–4 fields total visible at once.
- The contact-gate should appear on **step 2 or 3**, never step 1. Earlier taps are buttons (no typing), so the only typing is the final contact info.

### BUILD RECOMMENDATIONS — Q2
- **[R7]** Implement the hero capture as a **multi-step quiz, step 1 in the hero** (DEMO: front-end only, but build the full multi-step UX so it *looks and feels* like the real converting pattern).
- **[R8]** **Step 1 = one-tap project-type chooser** (Storm/Leak · Replacement · Inspection · Other) — buttons, no text entry, big tap targets (≥48px height).
- **[R9]** **Step 2 = ZIP + urgency** ("Is it actively leaking?" Yes/No; ZIP input). ZIP doubles as the service-area personalization hook.
- **[R10]** **Step 3 = contact-gate**: Name + Phone (required), Email (optional). This is the only step with text fields. Submit button verb = outcome ("Get My Free Inspection"), never "Submit."
- **[R11]** Show a **progress indicator** (Step 1 of 3 / segmented bar) — progressive disclosure works because the user sees the end is near.
- **[R12]** Provide a **parallel "or just call" affordance** at every step (click-to-call) so the call-now segment is never trapped in the form.
- **[R13]** Keep total fields ≤ ~5 and the contact-gate to 2–3 fields. Do not add address, roof sq-ft, budget dropdowns, etc. ATF — those are friction.
- **[R14]** Because it is a DEMO with no backend: on final submit, render a convincing **success state** ("Thanks — a Summit & Oak specialist will call you within the hour") so the transaction feels complete.

## Q3 — TRUST / PROOF: badges, star rating + review count, placement & density

### What actually lifts conversion
- **Reviews + badges + CTA in one layout can "double conversions overnight"** (mechanism: cognitive ease → emotional comfort → action).
- **Specific embedded reviews beat generic badges: +15–30%** when they carry first name + last initial + neighborhood + date.
- **Star rating + high review volume** is the core trust signal: aim **4.5★+ with high volume (300+).** Summit & Oak's "4.9 / 312 reviews" target fits this exactly. Round, large counts also read as harder-to-fake.
- **Badges that move the needle:** Google rating/Google Guaranteed, BBB Accredited, GAF (Master Elite / manufacturer cert), Owens Corning Preferred, license #. These "communicate safety and professionalism instantly."
- **License + insurance visibility** is table stakes for cold visitors who don't know you — a homeowner explicitly checks for licensed + insured.

### WHERE proof sits relative to CTAs
- **Adjacent to the primary CTA, not in a remote section.** The converting pattern brackets the action with proof: rating line directly above/below the form/CTA; badge row in the same hero block.
- Pipeline On's ATF priority literally lists "trust signals: Google rating, license number, years in business" as element #4, in the hero — and "license badge + crew photo below hero" as the next reinforcement.
- Proof should **repeat near every CTA** down the page (the rating line/badge strip re-appears next to secondary CTAs), because each CTA is a fresh decision moment.

### Proof density (premium restraint)
- ATF: **one rating line + one compact badge row (3–5 badges max).** Not a wall of 12 logos.
- A **2–3 review snippet strip** can sit just below the fold (or in the hero on desktop if room), then the full Google-reviews widget (Q4) further down.
- Density rule: enough to create cognitive ease, sparse enough to keep "clean premium, not visually dense." Trade logo *quantity* for logo *quality* + a strong rating line.

### NC COMPLIANCE GUARDRAILS (proof copy)
- **No** "we waive your deductible," "we act as your public adjuster," "guaranteed insurance approval."
- **No unattributed statistics** — any number shown must be attributable (e.g., the review count ties to the Google widget; "years in business" ties to the company). Avoid free-floating "98% approval"-style stats.
- Frame insurance help as *assistance with the claims process*, never as guaranteeing outcomes or handling the adjuster role.

### BUILD RECOMMENDATIONS — Q3
- **[R15]** Render a **rating line component** ("4.9 ★★★★★ · 312 Google reviews") in the hero, directly adjacent to the form/CTA. Use the gold/amber star color users associate with Google (#FBBC04-family) so it reads as authentic; review count in Plus Jakarta Sans.
- **[R16]** Add a **compact badge row** in the hero (≤5): Google rating, BBB Accredited, GAF/manufacturer, License # (as a text micro-badge "NC Lic. #XXXXX"), Insured. Monochrome/duotone treatment so they sit cleanly against charcoal without looking like clip-art.
- **[R17]** **Repeat the rating line + a 2-badge mini-strip next to every secondary CTA** down the page (services CTA, financing CTA, footer CTA).
- **[R18]** Place a **3-card review snippet strip** immediately below the hero (name + last initial + neighborhood + date + 1–2 line quote), feeding into the full reviews widget (Q4) further down.
- **[R19]** Make license/insured **explicit and specific** ("Licensed & Insured · NC Lic. #—") — table stakes for cold visitors.
- **[R20]** **Compliance lint:** ensure no deductible-waiver / public-adjuster / guaranteed-approval language anywhere in proof or hero copy, and every displayed statistic has a visible attribution. (DEMO data is fabricated but must still *read* as attributed, e.g., tied to the Google widget.)

## Q4 — PLACEHOLDER GOOGLE-REVIEWS WIDGET (exact anatomy to mock convincingly)

A real embedded Google reviews widget (per EmbedSocial / Trustindex / Taggbox conventions) has a consistent anatomy. To make the DEMO mock convincing, replicate **all** of these parts:

### 1. Header / summary block (the trust headline)
- **Google "G" logo** (the 4-color G) at left, or the full "Google" wordmark.
- **Aggregate score** large: e.g., **"4.9"** in big type.
- **Star row** rendering the average (5 gold stars, last partially filled if non-integer). Google star gold ≈ **#FBBC04 / #FFC107** range; empty/track stars light grey (#DADCE0).
- **Total review count** as a link-styled line: **"Based on 312 reviews"** or "312 Google reviews."
- Often a **"Write a review" / "Review us on Google" button** (Google-blue #4285F4 outline or fill).

### 2. Rating distribution bars (optional but adds realism)
- Five horizontal bars labeled **5★ → 1★**, each a filled track showing the proportion, with a count or % at the right. Gold/amber fill on a light grey track. For a 4.9, the 5★ bar is nearly full, 4★ small, 3–1★ tiny.

### 3. Individual review cards (the body)
Each card contains, in this layout:
- **Reviewer avatar** — a circular photo OR a colored circle with the reviewer's first initial (Google's auto-avatar style; assign varied flat colors).
- **Reviewer name** (e.g., "Sarah M.") in medium weight.
- **Small Google "G" glyph** in the card corner (signals the review's source).
- **Star row** for that review (almost all 5★).
- **Relative date** — "2 weeks ago," "a month ago," "3 days ago" (Google never shows absolute dates in the widget).
- **Review text** — 2–5 lines; realistic specifics (storm/leak, crew name, neighborhood, timeliness). Long ones truncate with a "Read more."
- **(Optional) Owner response** — an indented "Response from the owner" block with its own relative date; adds strong authenticity (real businesses reply).

### 4. Layout container
- **Carousel** (horizontal auto-scroll) is the canonical hero/landing-page format; **grid** (2–4 columns) for a dedicated section. Cards: white background, ~8–12px radius, subtle border/shadow, consistent padding.

### 5. Attribution footer
- **"Powered by Google"** or a small "Google" attribution + logo at the bottom edge of the widget — this single detail is the biggest "this is real" tell. Many widgets also show "See all reviews" linking out.

### Authenticity tells to include (so it doesn't look fake)
- **Relative dates**, never absolute.
- **Auto-initial avatars** mixed with a few photo avatars.
- **At least one owner response.**
- **Varied review lengths** + a couple of 4★ (not all 5★ — all-5 reads suspicious).
- **The 4-color Google G** rendered correctly.
- **"Powered by Google"** attribution.

### BUILD RECOMMENDATIONS — Q4
- **[R21]** Build a **`<GoogleReviewsWidget>`** component with three regions: (a) **summary header** (4-color G + "4.9" + gold star row + "Based on 312 reviews" + "Write a review" button), (b) optional **distribution bars** (5★→1★), (c) **review carousel** of cards.
- **[R22]** Each **review card**: circular avatar (initial-on-color or photo), "Sarah M.", corner Google G, 5-star row, **relative date** ("2 weeks ago"), 2–4 line quote with local specificity (mention Raleigh/Cary neighborhoods, storm/leak, crew professionalism, on-time).
- **[R23]** Use **authentic Google styling**: star gold ~#FBBC04, empty stars #DADCE0, Google blue #4285F4 for the write-review button, white cards on the page background.
- **[R24]** Include a **"Powered by Google" footer** + "See all 312 reviews" link styling — the single most important authenticity cue.
- **[R25]** Seed **10–15 fabricated-but-realistic reviews**: ~85% 5★, a couple 4★, **at least one owner response**, varied lengths, varied relative dates ("3 days ago"→"5 months ago").
- **[R26]** DEMO honesty: this is a mockup with no live Google data — make it *look* embedded (carousel auto-advance, hover states) but it does not need to link anywhere real. Keep names plausibly anonymized (first name + last initial), matching Google's actual display convention.

## Q5 — CLICK-TO-CALL & PHONE PROMINENCE (desktop + mobile, sticky patterns, measured lift)

### Why phone is priority #1 for this lane
- **Phone leads convert ~46%** vs ~7.8% all-channel — nearly **6×.** For storm/emergency (panic, "water dripping right now"), the call is the conversion.
- Every page needs **≥1 prominent call CTA above the fold.** Top performers show the number **twice ATF** — once in the header, once as a hero CTA.

### Desktop patterns
- **Phone number in the header**, top-right, as a tappable `tel:` link with a phone icon + the number rendered legibly (homeowners read it aloud / dial manually on desktop).
- **Sticky header** that keeps the number visible on scroll.
- **Click-to-call as a co-primary hero action** (Pattern C dual-action, or a "Call (919)…" secondary button beside the form CTA).

### Mobile patterns (71% of traffic)
- **Sticky bottom call bar** — tap-to-call + a "Book/Get Estimate" button pinned to the bottom of the viewport. **Measured lift: +25–40% call volume** (Pipeline On). This is one of the highest-ROI mobile elements available.
- **Header click-to-call** in addition to the sticky bar.
- **Tap targets ≥ 44×44px** (Apple HIG / accessibility standard); Pipeline On says ≥48px height for the primary button.
- `tel:` protocol so a tap opens the dialer instantly (no copy/paste).

### BUILD RECOMMENDATIONS — Q5
- **[R27]** **Desktop header:** tappable `tel:` number, top-right, phone icon + number in Plus Jakarta Sans, contractor-red (#D8262C) accent or button; persists in a **sticky header** on scroll.
- **[R28]** **Hero co-primary:** render the phone as a second action beside the form CTA (Pattern C) OR as an "or call (919) ___-____" line directly under the form — so the call-now segment converts without scrolling.
- **[R29]** **Mobile sticky call bar (REQUIRED):** fixed bottom bar, two zones — left **"Call Now"** (`tel:`, red) + right **"Free Estimate"** (opens/scrolls to quiz). Height ≥56px, tap targets ≥48px, safe-area-inset padding for notched phones. This is the +25–40% lever.
- **[R30]** All phone elements use **`<a href="tel:+1919…">`**; never an image of a number. Tap targets ≥44px everywhere.
- **[R31]** Show the number **twice ATF** (header + hero) per the top-performer pattern; repeat in footer.
- **[R32]** Style the sticky bar to respect the premium palette (charcoal bar, red primary, white type) and add a subtle entrance (slide-up) — but keep it always-present, not scroll-gated away.

## Q6 — FITTING ALL 5 DECISION INPUTS ATF WITHOUT CLUTTER (premium restraint)

The 5 decision inputs: **(1) book-an-estimate, (2) rating/reviews, (3) price range, (4) financing, (5) proof.** Cold homeowners want all five answered before they act — but "clean premium, not visually dense" forbids cramming. The resolution: **collapse each input to its smallest honest token** and let hero photo + whitespace carry the premium feel.

### The token map (each input → minimal ATF expression)
| Decision input | Smallest ATF token | Placement |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Book-an-estimate | The multi-step quiz step-1 (one-tap) **+** single dominant CTA | Hero form column / centered card |
| 2. Rating / reviews | One line: "4.9 ★ · 312 Google reviews" | Directly adjacent to the CTA |
| 3. Price range | Implied via financing token + "free inspection/estimate" (no scary $8k–20k number up top) | Near CTA |
| 4. Financing | One line / chip: "From $X/mo with approved financing*" | Under the CTA |
| 5. Proof | Compact badge row (GAF/BBB/License/Insured) + crew photo | Hero block / photo |

### Why this clears the clutter bar
- **Single dominant CTA** (not 4 competing buttons) is itself the biggest de-clutter + the biggest conversion lever (+371% clicks figure). Click-to-call is the only co-action.
- Each proof element is **one line or one small row**, not a section. The rating line, financing line, and license micro-line are *text*, near-zero visual weight.
- The **hero photo + charcoal scrim + generous whitespace** does the "premium" work; the tokens layer on top without fighting it.
- **Price** stays implied (financing "from $X/mo" + "free estimate") rather than a blunt 5-figure number — honest, less scary, less cluttered.

### Premium restraint rules
- Max **2 actions** in hero (form/CTA + call). Max **5 badges.** Max **one** rating line, **one** financing line.
- Whitespace > density. "Full, not overstuffed": the eye should land on the headline, then the action, then catch the proof tokens peripherally.
- Typography hierarchy carries order: Newsreader display headline (largest), Plus Jakarta body for sub/lines, smallest weight for license micro-line + financing asterisk.

### BUILD RECOMMENDATIONS — Q6
- **[R33]** Lay the hero out so **all 5 tokens are present but each is minimal**: headline (Newsreader) → sub → **rating line** → **quiz step-1 / CTA** → **financing line** → **badge row**, over a real crew photo with charcoal scrim. (Desktop: split; mobile: stacked, all within first viewport.)
- **[R34]** Express **price as financing + "free estimate"** ("From $X/mo · Free inspection"), not a raw $8k–20k figure ATF — keeps it inviting and uncluttered while still answering "what's this cost."
- **[R35]** Enforce **single-dominant-CTA discipline**: one primary action button + click-to-call co-primary; nothing else competes ATF.
- **[R36]** Use **whitespace and type scale**, not boxes-in-boxes, to separate the tokens — avoid card-in-card density; let the scrim'd photo be the only "container."
- **[R37]** Add a compliance asterisk on financing ("*with approved credit; example payment, terms vary") to satisfy NC rules while keeping the line short.
- **[R38]** QA the "no-clutter" goal with a 5-second-test mindset: at a glance, a user should be able to name the company's offer, see they're rated, and find the action — if the hero feels busy, cut a badge or move the review strip below the fold.

## UNCERTAIN / CONFLICTING

- **Exact magnitude of multi-step lift varies wildly by source** (+61%, +86%, +206%, "up to 300%"). Direction is consistent (multi-step > single flat form for lead-gen), but treat the specific percentage as illustrative, not a guarantee. The contractor-specific figure (+30–60% on mobile, Pipeline On) is the most relevant anchor for roofing.
- **Form-in-hero vs multi-step are not mutually exclusive** — the strongest play (and our recommendation) is *both*: a multi-step quiz whose first step lives in the hero. The +15% ClickMechanic figure was for moving *a* form into the hero (not specifically a quiz), so combining them is an informed synthesis, not a directly-tested number.
- **"Double conversions overnight" from reviews+badges+CTA** comes from agency/vendor sources (yourlocalsitedesign), not a controlled study — directionally credible (matches the specific-reviews +15–30% figure) but the "double" magnitude is marketing-grade, not peer-reviewed.
- **The +371% clicks / +1,617% revenue single-CTA stat** is a widely-circulated marketing figure (originally WordStream-lineage); the direction (fewer CTAs convert better) is well-supported, the exact numbers are not independently verified here.
- **Some sources are vendor/agency blogs** (widget makers, roofing-marketing agencies) with an incentive to overstate. The eye-tracking/fold claims (NN/g) and the channel-conversion claims (phone 46%, home-services 7.8%) are the most authoritative; the contractor landing-page benchmark stack (3-5% median / 8% top-quartile / 12% top-decile) appears consistently across independent sources and aligns with the project's 8–14% ceiling target.
- **Price-range ATF is a judgment call.** Data supports showing *financing* ATF (12–20% lift) but is silent on showing a raw price range; surfacing a 5-figure number up top plausibly *hurts* (sticker shock). Recommendation (price-as-financing + free-estimate) is reasoned synthesis, flagged as not directly A/B-sourced.
- **Google widget exact spec** is reverse-engineered from third-party widget makers (EmbedSocial, Trustindex, Taggbox) and Google's GBP display conventions, not an official Google design spec. The anatomy described is what real widgets render and will read as authentic, but Google does not publish an official embeddable-widget styleguide.
