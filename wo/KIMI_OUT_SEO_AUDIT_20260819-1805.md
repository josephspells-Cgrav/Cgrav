  ## ANSWERS

  **1. Doorway analysis.** The "clear" verdict is *lexically* sound but *semantically* wrong-headed. Jaccard on 5-word shingles cannot detect the thing Google's doorway definition actually targets — pages that exist to catch query variants while funneling to the same value proposition — and the artifact concedes exactly that condition in plain language ("the same things in different words"). The chrome-inclusive measure biases similarity upward, so 33% is a conservative read and these pages are genuinely not town-swap templates; that much holds. What's unsound is the implied license: "80–95%" and "≤25%" are invented thresholds, and "Google ranks them" is weak exculpation (ranking at #43–55 is what suppression looks like). The plan crosses the line at a specific point: **A6 running autonomously on public data without S2**, and any future service×city matrix generated before S1/S2 exist. The line is not a similarity percentage — it's whether each page carries first-party evidence (real jobs, photos, reviews, named neighborhoods of actual work) or interchangeable public-data filler. As written, A6 permits the filler.

  **2. GBP/pack strategy.** Wrong on the physics. The data supports "Durham pack unwinnable *this year*" (200–328-review incumbents), but the pivot to Cary/Garner/Apex/Holly Springs packs is not winnable *at all* from a Durham address — those towns are ~18–35 miles out, pack ranking is distance-gated, and neither reviews naming a town nor job history in it moves the pin. The plan concedes the only market where proximity is an asset because its fixable deficit (reviews) is large, and targets markets whose deficit (distance) is unfixable. Correct read: secondary towns are an **organic** play (location pages — which the artifact already has ranking #43–55 and which don't need proximity); the pack play is the Durham-radius review-velocity war it names but deprioritizes, plus configuring GBP service areas. A Durham-addressed GBP can win borderline searches near the county line (RTP/Brier Creek edge) — that's the realistic secondary-pack ceiling.

  **3. Ceiling model.** The arithmetic is internally consistent and the provenance honesty (DERIVED, n flagged, falsifiable milestone) is genuinely good — it does not materially mislead *as a bound*. Weakest single link: **the 6-month position assumptions** (Durham #5–10, Cary #3–8, 20–30 informational top-10s, from a base of best-#19/zero-top-10) — every downstream number scales off them, and the plan under-resources the one input that moves commercial positions (authority: one sponsorship link + directories). Secondary weakness: 2/37 conversion used at 2–5% (fine as conservative, but the CI is 1.5–18% — the "15–45 leads" 12-month row is two shaky multiplications deep). The 3–5× seasonality is directionally right and correctly framed as a bound. The "8–25 leads/mo at 6mo" *will* be read as a forecast by an operator despite the hedge; the fix is F14's leading-indicator checkpoint.
