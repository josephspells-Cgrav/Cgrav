A design-system research brief for the firm's own site. Every section gives concrete techniques, named exemplars with URLs, and do/don't rules you can encode. Anti-slop and opinionated throughout.

## 1. Gold-on-black executed as EXPENSIVE, not guru/casino

**The single governing principle (from luxury color doctrine):** gold must *serve* the black, never compete with it. "Luxury isn't about the color — it's about restraint. Fewer colors = higher perceived value." Black carries 60–70% of the visual weight; gold is an accent only.

**The hex discipline that separates premium from cheap:**
- Anchor on a true near-black (#0A0A0A is the canonical luxury onyx — you already have this).
- The luxury palette guide explicitly warns: **avoid "web gold" #FFD700 — "it reads neon and cheap" / "signals discount."** Your locked #ffb900 is brighter/more saturated than the classic luxury gold (#C69B3C / #D4AF37). This is the single biggest risk in your kit. Mitigation that keeps your bold-gold identity while dodging the "casino" read:
  - Reserve full-saturation #ffb900 for the *smallest, highest-intent* marks only (CTA, the win-line on the chart, the active nav state, the logo crest). Use #856709 (your deep gold) and a desaturated mid-gold for larger gold passages so the bright gold stays a scarce "ping."
  - Never fill a large area with bright gold. "Full-page gold fills read as kitsch." Gold belongs on type, the monogram, seals/badges, thin rules, the one data line — not on panels, hero backgrounds, or big buttons-as-blocks.

**Where gold is ALLOWED vs FORBIDDEN (encode this as a rule):**
- ✅ Allowed: logo/crest, headline accent words, the win-line on the dashboard, 1px hairline rules/dividers, small seals/credential badges ("143 sites scrubbed"), active/hover states, numerals in stats, eyebrow micro-labels.
- ❌ Forbidden: large gold fills, gold gradients (the luxury rule: gold "cannot reproduce in foil/embroidery as a gradient" — and gradient-gold is the #1 hustle-guru tell), two metallics together (gold + silver/chrome = "visual competition"), and the **black + red + gold triad simultaneously** which "reads as a casino menu." You have a burnt-terracotta #c25a3a — keep it genuinely *rare* and never adjacent to a gold CTA, or you'll trip the casino read.

**Metallic vs flat:** keep gold FLAT in the digital surface. Skip glossy bevels, drop-shadow "shine," and faux-3D gold chrome — those are the crypto/casino signature. If you want a hint of "metal," do it with a single very subtle linear sheen on the logo lockup *only* (one element, static), not on UI gold.

**Named exemplars (study these for the restraint mechanism):**
- **Lamborghini** — `lamborghini.com` — true-black surfaces, gold accents used only on type and small marks, dramatic uppercase. The reference for "dark cathedral + scarce gold."
- **Rolls-Royce Motor Cars** — `rolls-royce.com` (Awwwards Honorable Mention) — product-as-lifestyle, vast negative space, bespoke "Rolls-Royce Pioneer" type; proves restraint + custom type = institutional.
- **Tom Ford** — `tomford.com` — black + gold + ivory, the formal luxury triad; near-zero color elsewhere.
- **Stripe** — `stripe.com` — not gold, but the master class in deep-background + scarce-accent + weight-300 elegance and generous margins; "deep navy + precise accent colors communicating stability."
- Heritage anchors for the black+gold *system* itself: Chanel, Rolex, Dom Pérignon — all run black 60–70% / gold-as-seal-only / one softening neutral.

**The "softening neutral" move:** the formal luxury standard is black + gold + **ivory**, not black + gold alone. Gold-on-pure-black-with-nothing-else is what reads thin/guru. Your warm-dark neutrals (#1f1b12 / #2d2516) plus white body text serve this role — use the warm-darks as panel/section surfaces so gold has somewhere to sit *other than* on pure black, and let white (not gold) carry the body load.

## 2. Grotesque type system at the premium tier

Typography is "the single fastest way to escape AI slop." But note a trap: **Space Grotesk + Instrument Serif is itself flagged as one of the most over-used AI-default combos.** Space Grotesk is fine as a *base*, but to read bespoke/institutional you must set it with intention and ideally pair it with a less-defaulted body face.

**The display treatment — "big confident headline":**
- **Set huge, set tight.** Large display type uses *tighter* letter-spacing (negative tracking, roughly −1% to −3% on big grotesque headlines) and *tight leading* (line-height ~0.95–1.05) to "feel compact and powerful." Loose tracking on a big headline is an instant amateur tell.
- **Weight contrast is the authority lever.** Pair a very heavy display weight (700–800, or go Druk-grade ultra-bold for hero statements) against a light/regular body. Druk (Commercial Type) is *the* editorial "make a statement" face — its uncompromising boldness is exactly the "firm that owns the standard" energy. Free-tier substitutes if you avoid licensing: **Archivo / Archivo Expanded** (your candidate) reads heavier and more architectural than Space Grotesk; **Anton** or a condensed grotesque for the loudest hero word.
- **Caps with control.** Lamborghini/luxury-auto authority comes from *dramatic uppercase* — but uppercase needs *positive* tracking (open it up ~+4–8%) or it clots. The rule: tight tracking on mixed-case display, open tracking on all-caps eyebrows/labels.

**The pairing for institutional voice:**
- Display: a confident grotesque set heavy and tight (Archivo / Druk-style / your "King Maker Grotesque").
- Body: don't run everything in one grotesque (mono-typeface everywhere is a slop tell). Founders Grotesk (Klim) is the premium pairing partner for Space Grotesk and gives early-20th-century grotesque authority; Graphik is the editorial workhorse ("low contrast, open counters, compact descenders… ideal for tight editorial settings"). A clean, slightly warmer body grotesque under a colder display grotesque creates the display-vs-body contrast that reads "designed," not "generated."
- One optional editorial serif accent is allowed — but use it for a *pull quote or a single proof line*, not as the AI-cliché italic-serif-hero-word (that exact move is on every slop checklist).

**Named type references:** Vercel commissioned **Geist** (bespoke = brand); Linear runs "custom-modified type reinforcing precision"; Stripe uses a "bespoke serif for headlines + clean sans for body." The lesson: the premium tier *customizes or carefully pairs* — it never ships one default face untouched. Even lightly customizing Space Grotesk (adjust the tracking, set your own optical sizes, lock a distinctive weight ramp) moves you off the default.

## 3. The anti-AI-slop checklist for dark/gold sites (the "10-second rattle")

This is the most actionable section. The consolidated list of tells that out a dark/gold site as AI-generated or hustle-guru — encode every one as a "never":

**The 16 canonical slop tells (Developers Digest + 925 Studios):**
1. **Inter everywhere** (or any untouched default face). ❌
2. **Over-used combos** — Space Grotesk + Instrument Serif, Geist defaults. (Relevant to you — customize.) ❌
3. **Serif-italic hero accent word** — the single italicized serif word in an otherwise sans hero. ❌
4. **"VibeCode purple" / lavender accent.** (You're gold — safe, but the lesson is "one signature color, owned.") 
5. **Permanent dark mode with medium-grey body text** that fails WCAG AA. ❌ Your body must be white/near-white, not grey-on-black.
6. **Gradient mush** — purple→blue (or here, gold→amber) background/text gradients. ❌ The gold-gradient is your specific casino risk.
7. **Colored glows and big colored box-shadows** — "glow on everything." ❌ This is THE dark-site guru tell. No gold glow behind cards, buttons, or headlines.
8. **Centered-everything hero** — centered generic-sans headline. ❌ Go asymmetric/editorial (left-aligned, magazine grid).
9. **Badge above the H1.** ❌
10. **Colored card borders / colored left-stripe on cards** — "almost as reliable a sign of AI design" as anything. ❌
11. **Identical icon-on-top feature cards** in a 3-up grid. ❌
12. **Numbered 1-2-3 step sequences.** ❌ (Or at least don't render them as the default chip-circles.)
13. **Stat-banner row** of evenly-spaced figures. ❌ as a generic strip — but *you need stats*; render them as designed data-viz (Section 5), not a flat banner.
14. **Emoji-icon sidebars / emoji bullets.** ❌ Absolutely no emoji on this site.
15. **All-caps section labels** used reflexively. (Allowed sparingly as eyebrows with proper tracking — overuse is the tell.)
16. **Uniform 16px radius + 24px padding everywhere; generic fade-ins everywhere.** ❌ — you've already chosen **square corners**, which is itself a strong anti-slop signal; keep it ruthless (0 radius, or a single tiny consistent value, never the default rounded-everything).

**Additional luxury-specific tells to forbid:**
- Stock "diverse-team-at-laptops" or "abstract 3D blob/gradient orb" imagery. Replace with real proof: dashboard screenshots, real audit data, the 143-site evidence. "Specificity signals authenticity AI cannot replicate."
- Vague hero copy ("Build the future of…", "Your all-in-one platform"). Your Howard-Roark declarative voice is the antidote — keep copy specific, opinionated, forgettable-proof.
- Faux-3D gold objects, floating gold particles, bokeh. Casino/crypto. ❌

**The three moves the cleanest 46% of sites make (do all three):** (1) one non-default, *owned* color palette with intent; (2) typefaces other than untouched Inter, with a distinct body face; (3) commit to ONE layout primitive repeated throughout rather than mixing card/section styles. For King Maker: gold-on-black owned + Archivo/Druk-grade display + Founders/Graphik body + one repeating editorial section primitive.

## 4. Premium MOTION that is also AI-LEGIBLE

**The AI-legibility constraint is load-bearing and overrides motion taste where they conflict.** Your own QA standard already proved per-letter inline-block text animation *fragments rendered extraction* (a crawler read "S t o r m  D a m a g e" off a competitor). So:

**Text reveals — WORD-LEVEL, never per-letter.**
- Animate at the word or line level via masked/clip-path reveals (`clip-path` is GPU-accelerated, S-tier). Wrap each word/line in a span and animate `transform: translateY()` + `opacity` behind an `overflow: hidden` mask — the "rise from behind a line" reveal that reads expensive.
- The DOM text stays intact and crawlable (opacity/transform are purely visual — "search engines still read and index normally"). Verify with a Playwright `innerText` assertion that rendered text is unbroken, N/N clean. Never rely on `aria-label` as the SEO fallback.

**Which properties to animate (Motion performance tier list — encode as law):**
- **S-tier (animate freely, stays 60/120fps even under main-thread load):** `transform`, `opacity`, `filter`, `clip-path`. Scroll-driven animation via native `scroll()` / View Timeline is also S-tier because scrolling runs on the compositor.
- **C-tier (avoid):** animating `background-color`, `color`, `border-radius`, SVG attrs — triggers paint. *Especially:* animating CSS custom properties triggers paint **+ style recalc across all inheriting elements** ("inheritance bomb," measured 8ms/frame on a real site). If you must animate a variable, use `@property` with `inherits: false`.
- **D/F-tier (never):** `width`, `height`, `margin`, `top/left` (layout thrash). Use `transform` instead.

**The motion vocabulary that signals "expensive firm" vs "template":**
- **Scroll-driven reveals + pinned sections** (GSAP ScrollTrigger or native scroll timelines): pin a section, scrub a sequence as the user scrolls. The award-tier patterns: staggered "blinds" reveals, scroll-driven stacking accordions (cards compress into a stacked deck), horizontal-scroll proof galleries, parallax depth between fore/background layers. Studios to study: **Active Theory** (`activetheory.net`), **Locomotive** (`locomotive.ca`), **Resn** (`resn.co.nz`), **Cuberto** (`cuberto.com`) — "motion that's deliberate, not decorative; every transition reinforces the studio's focus."
- **Restraint = premium.** Stripe's motion "feels precise and mathematical." That's your register — measured, mechanical, confident. Not bouncy, not playful. Start with micro-interactions on CTAs/inputs only; remove purely decorative motion. "Generic fade-ins everywhere" is a slop tell — make each reveal deliberate.
- **Marquee/ticker** (logo wall, proof-points strip): use a reprojection-style ticker that clones only the minimum items, runs only while visible (battery/GPU friendly). Caveat from the tier list: huge layers like full-width marquees can exhaust mobile GPU budget — keep the animated layer small.
- **Count-up data-viz**: animate numerals on enter-viewport (S-tier transform/opacity, or a dedicated AnimateNumber-style component). This is how the dashboard "proof" lands.

**Reduced-motion (non-negotiable):** wrap everything in `gsap.matchMedia()` / `prefers-reduced-motion` guards; the best ticker/animation libs respect reduced-motion *mandatorily and non-configurably*. Reduced-motion path = final state shown instantly, content fully present. This protects both accessibility and AI-legibility (a crawler/headless-with-reduced-motion sees the complete text).

## 5. "Dashboard-as-proof" / data-viz that reads as real evidence

This is your differentiator — the firm's own win-line as exhibit A. How to make it read as *proof*, not decoration:

**Make it read REAL, not stock:**
- Premium dark-dashboard pattern: near-black canvas, **a single accent color**, high-contrast text. "Restraint makes dark interfaces feel premium rather than muddy." That's exactly your gold-on-black — the win-line in bright #ffb900, every baseline/competitor line in muted warm-grey (#856709 desaturated or a low-opacity neutral). The eye goes straight to the gold line because nothing else competes — this *is* "the win-line pops gold against muted baselines."
- Use REAL data the firm can defend: organic-ranking trajectory, the 143-site audit distribution, ranking position over 90 days. Specificity is the anti-AI move ("Loom shows actual recordings, Amplitude shows dashboard data" — real screenshots beat illustrations).
- **Progressive disclosure:** surface the one number that answers "did it work?" first (the headline win), then let the detail sit below for the believers. Don't dump a wall of KPIs.

**Charting craft for the premium read:**
- One bright line + muted baselines (never a rainbow of series). Semantic color: gold = the win, neutral-grey = the field, the rare terracotta only for a "before/decline" reference if needed.
- Thin, precise gridlines (very low-opacity white), generous chart margins, mono or tabular-figure numerals for the axis labels and stat read-outs (tabular figures stop numbers from "wiggling" during count-up — a subtle premium tell).
- Count-up the headline metric on scroll-in; draw the gold line with a `clip-path`/stroke-reveal (S-tier) left-to-right as the section pins.
- Render it as a *designed* artifact framed in a warm-dark panel (#1f1b12) with a 1px gold hairline top-border — so it reads as an instrument, not a screenshot dropped in.

**Named references for premium data-viz:** **Vercel Observability** (`vercel.com/products/observability`) and **Linear** — dark canvas, single-accent, high-contrast, real telemetry rendered as the hero proof. The B2B-SaaS dashboard convention: "the right data visible before anyone clicks; the one metric that answers 'is everything okay?' first."

**Avoid:** the flat horizontal "stat banner row" of evenly-spaced figures (slop tell #13) — that's the cheap version of proof. Same numbers, but composed into a designed chart/instrument instead of a generic strip.
