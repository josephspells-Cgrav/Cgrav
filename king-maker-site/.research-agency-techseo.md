## 1. SCHEMA.ORG — the agency @graph

### Type choice (the load-bearing decision)
Use **`Organization`** as the single canonical firm node — NOT `ProfessionalService` as the primary type. The 2026 consensus moved away from `ProfessionalService` as a catch-all: it blurred "the business as entity" with "the service as offer." Model those as separate nodes.

- **Firm entity** → `Organization` (or `ProfessionalService` only if you want the LocalBusiness-lineage props like address/openingHours AND you operate a real physical office; `ProfessionalService` is a subtype of `LocalBusiness`, so only use it if local-pack/NAP signals matter. For a national B2B firm, plain `Organization` is cleaner and avoids implying local-business intent). **Recommendation: `Organization` is the right call for King Maker** — it's national-authority positioning, not a local office play. Note `Organization` produces NO standalone rich result, but it is THE foundational entity-trust signal for Knowledge Panel + AI Mode citation.
- **Services offered** → `Service` nodes, wired to the firm via `provider`, optionally bundled in an `OfferCatalog` via `hasOfferCatalog`, each with a nested `Offer`.
- **Founder/team** → `Person` nodes with `worksFor` → `#org` and `knowsAbout` (topical expertise — feeds E-E-A-T).
- **Pages** → `WebSite` + per-page `WebPage`, plus `BreadcrumbList`.

### The valid @graph (single JSON-LD block, `@id`-linked)
```
@graph:
- Organization  @id #org    → name, url, logo (ImageObject), sameAs[], foundingDate,
                               knowsAbout[], founder→#founder, areaServed
- WebSite       @id #website → url, publisher→#org, name
- Person        @id #founder → name, jobTitle, worksFor→#org, knowsAbout[], sameAs[]
- Service       @id #svc-seo → name "Enterprise Authority Websites + SEO",
                               serviceType, provider→#org, areaServed,
                               hasOfferCatalog→OfferCatalog{ itemListElement:[Offer→Service]}
- WebPage       @id #webpage → isPartOf→#website, about→#org, breadcrumb→#breadcrumb,
                               primaryImageOfPage, speakable (answer pages only)
- BreadcrumbList @id #breadcrumb → itemListElement[] (final/current item OMITS `item`)
```
`@id` linking is the whole game: one canonical `#org` node at a stable fragment URI (e.g. `https://kingmaker.com/#org`), referenced from every page's graph by `{"@id":"…#org"}` rather than re-declared. This is what lets Google/AI triangulate a single entity across the whole site instead of seeing N disconnected businesses.

### `sameAs` — the single highest-leverage entity signal in 2026
`sameAs[]` on the Organization node is described as "the single most important entity signal." Point it at every authoritative profile that agrees on your identity: **Wikidata (most powerful — it's a direct Knowledge Graph input), LinkedIn company page, Crunchbase, G2/Clutch, X, YouTube**. The more sources that agree, the higher your entity-confidence score → better Knowledge Panel accuracy AND more AI-Mode citations. Add `knowsAbout[]` (declare your expertise topics) on both `Organization` and founder `Person`.

### 2026 RICH-RESULT GROUND TRUTH — alive vs dead
⚠️ **This is the part most "schema guides" get wrong now.**

| Type | 2026 Status | Action |

## 2. llms.txt + AI-LEGIBILITY (2026)

### Honest ground-truth on llms.txt (don't oversell it)
⚠️ **llms.txt is near-zero ROI for AI-search citations today, and you should know that before building the pitch around it.**
- Adoption ~**10%** of domains (SE Ranking, 300k-domain study).
- **Google confirmed (July 2025, Gary Illyes) it does NOT and will NOT support llms.txt.**
- Across 500M+ AI-bot visits, only ~408 requests targeted llms.txt directly; in one 62,100-visit domain, 84 hits = **0.1% of AI crawler traffic.** GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, OAI-SearchBot, Google-Extended overwhelmingly **skip it and crawl HTML directly.**
- 8 of 9 sites saw no measurable traffic change after adding it.

**Where it DOES earn its place:** the **agentic/dev layer** — Stripe, Vercel, Cloudflare, Anthropic, Coinbase ship it because coding agents (Cursor, etc.) read it. For an agency that sells "Kingmaker Agentic OS," **shipping a clean llms.txt is a credibility/signaling move** ("the site is the proof — we did the thing the category ignores"), not a traffic lever. Build it, position it as proof, don't claim it drives rankings.

### llms.txt format (if you ship it — and you should, as proof)
- Plain markdown at root `/llms.txt`. One-paragraph authoritative firm summary up top, then curated `## section` lists of your highest-value URLs (service pages, the 143-site audit, pillar guides, company facts) with one-line descriptions. Omit thin/marketing pages.
- Optional `/llms-full.txt` = full expanded content for agents.

### What ACTUALLY makes you AI-legible (the real lever)
This is where the effort goes — because crawlers read **rendered HTML**, not your curated file:
1. **Server-render real text.** AI extractors parse HTML/rendered innerText. ⚠️ Your own memory already flags this: per-letter inline-block heading animations FRAGMENT rendered extraction (a crawler read "S t o r m  D a m a g e" off a sister site). **Animate at WORD level / mask / clip-path — never per-character — and never rely on `aria-label` as the SEO fallback.** Verify across static source + rendered innerText + an actual AI extraction. This applies double here since the firm's site IS the proof.
2. **Answer-first content** — front-load the answer in the opening 1-2 lines, then evidence. AI engines extract the lead.
3. **Citation-friendly structure** — statistics, cited sources, and direct quotations are the three content moves with the strongest measured citation lift. (King Maker's "143 sites scrubbed" stat is exactly this.)
4. **TL;DR blocks, Q&A sections, data tables** — extractable chunks.
5. **Visible author + datePublished + dateModified.** Freshness is huge: content updated within 30 days gets **3.2× more AI citations** than stale content.
6. **`robots.txt` must ALLOW AI bots** (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended) if you want citations — the most common own-goal.

Scale context: AI search ≈ **12-18% of English informational queries Q1 2026** (was <2% a year ago) — this is no longer optional. Note ChatGPT pulls ~48% of citations from Wikipedia and Perplexity ~47% from community/Reddit — so an **entity-strong Wikidata/`sameAs` presence (Topic 1) is your inroad to ChatGPT citations.**

## 3. CORE WEB VITALS — motion-heavy premium Next.js, kept green

### 2026 thresholds (stricter, INP is the killer)
LCP **≤2.5s** · INP **≤200ms** · CLS **≤0.1**. **INP (replaced FID) is the most-failed metric — 43% of sites fail 200ms.** For a GSAP/Framer site this is the one to obsess over, because animation engines run on the main thread.

### LCP techniques
- **Exclude the LCP element from lazy-load.** Hero image/text must be in the initial HTML, not lazy-loaded — lazy-loading the LCP element is a classic regression. Use `next/image` with `priority` on the hero.
- **`next/image`** auto-serves AVIF/WebP, sets width/height (kills CLS), responsive sizes.
- ⚠️ **Hidden App-Router LCP trap:** a page-level `"use client"` ships the whole tree as client JS and hydrates before paint. **Push `"use client"` down to the smallest interactive leaf.** Keep the hero/above-fold as a Server Component.

### CLS techniques
- Reserve space for ALL media (aspect-ratio boxes), embeds, and animated elements — never animate layout properties (width/height/top/left); animate `transform`/`opacity` only.
- **Font CLS:** `next/font` self-hosts and injects `size-adjust` + `ascent/descent/line-gap-override` so the fallback renders at the real font's metrics — **eliminates swap CLS.** Use ONE **variable font** (Space Grotesk / Archivo per the brand) with a constrained weight range: a variable file beats 2+ static weights every time, and a single variable font avoids the Chromium font-metric reflow that resets the LCP candidate. `display: swap`, `subsets: ['latin']`.

### INP techniques (the hard one for motion sites)
- **Bundle reality:** GSAP core ~67KB, +ScrollTrigger/ScrollSmoother/MorphSVG easily >150KB; Framer Motion full >90KB. All parses/executes on the main thread. **Motion.dev (`motion`) is ~8KB core and up to 2.5× faster than GSAP** at animating unknown DOM values (deferred keyframe resolution) — strongly consider it for a site where INP is a hard gate, OR scope GSAP imports tightly.
- **Code-split animation libs; init only on pages that need them**; defer init to after interactive.
- ⚠️ **`will-change: transform` left on after an animation completes, across many elements, causes memory pressure → INP spikes on later interactions.** Add it before, remove it after.
- Break long tasks into chunks; keep handlers light.
- **RSC + progressive hydration:** non-interactive sections as Server Components ship zero JS and skip hydration entirely — frees the main thread. Group interactive areas in `Suspense` so they hydrate independently → lower INP.
- `gsap.matchMedia()` + **`prefers-reduced-motion`** (also an accessibility/axe gate).

### Next.js 16 specifics (released Oct 2025)
- **Turbopack is now the default** for `next dev` AND `next build` (2-5× faster builds, 5-10× faster Fast Refresh). ⚠️ Production builds still use Webpack under the hood in some paths; full Turbopack-prod lands later 2026.
- **PPR (Partial Prerendering) is STABLE** — opt in via `cacheComponents: true`. In 16, **code is dynamic by default**; you opt INTO static caching with the `"use cache"` directive or by structuring with `Suspense`. PPR serves a static HTML shell instantly from CDN and streams dynamic "holes" — ideal: the hero/above-fold ships as a static shell (great LCP) while any dynamic proof-widget streams in. For a mostly-static marketing site, mark the shell `"use cache"` so you keep full-SSG speed.
- Measure with **field data** (CrUX/RUM), not just lab — INP especially only shows up under real interaction.

## 4. TOPICAL AUTHORITY / CONTENT HUB (B2B firm)

### The architecture (three-tier silo)
- **Tier 1 — Core Pillars:** 3,000-5,000-word comprehensive guides defining the firm's primary expertise (e.g. "Authority Websites vs. GoHighLevel Brochures," "Organic Regional Dominance for Home-Service Contractors").
- **Tier 2 — Supporting Clusters:** 1,500-2,500-word articles each going DEEPER on one subtopic than the pillar's section on it.
- **Tier 3 — Supporting pages:** case studies (the 143-site audit), service pages, definitions.

### The ranking mechanics
- **A site with ~20 interconnected articles on a topic consistently outranks a single 5,000-word guide** — even a technically superior one. Correctly-built clusters average **+40% organic traffic**, some 50-300% in 6-12 months.
- **Internal-link silo:** pages link mostly within their topic group and all point back to the pillar. This concentrates topical signal and lets Google identify what each section is "about." (⚠️ Also satisfies your own no-orphan rule — every hub page must be ≤2 clicks from nav, not just built.)
- ☠️ **Anti-pattern: thin cluster pages are WORSE than none** — if a cluster page doesn't out-cover the pillar's section on that subtopic, it's cannibalization, not authority. Quality gate every cluster page.

### E-E-A-T for a firm (2026)
- **Named experts with persistent bylines** — every pillar/cluster authored by a small set of named `Person` entities whose bylines accumulate authority over time. Wire each to the `Person` schema node (Topic 1) with `knowsAbout`.
- Same technical depth for the algorithm as for a C-suite reader (B2B).
- **Topical authority drives AI citation:** ChatGPT/Perplexity/AI Overviews preferentially cite sources they recognize as consistently authoritative on a topic — so the hub doubles as your GEO engine. This is the direct bridge between Topic 4 and Topic 2.

## 5. METADATA / OG / CANONICAL / SITEMAP / ROBOTS — Next.js 16 SSG checklist

The Next.js **Metadata API is the single source of truth** — server-rendered into tags during render. Full "passes-its-own-audit" checklist:

**Per-route metadata**
- [ ] `export const metadata` (static pages) OR `generateMetadata()` (dynamic/[slug]) on every route — never hand-rolled `<head>` tags.
- [ ] `title` (template via `title.template` in root layout, e.g. `%s | King Maker`) + unique `description` per page.
- [ ] **Canonical** via `alternates: { canonical: '<absolute-url>' }` on EVERY page — explicit, absolute, self-referential. (Most common SSG audit miss.)
- [ ] `metadataBase` set in root layout so OG/canonical resolve to absolute URLs.

**Open Graph / Twitter**
- [ ] `openGraph` (title, description, url, siteName, type, images) + `twitter` (card: `summary_large_image`). Remember: nested OG/robots in a child segment OVERWRITE the parent — set them per-route deliberately.
- [ ] **OG images:** use the file-convention `opengraph-image.tsx` (dynamic, per-route) or static `opengraph-image.png` (1200×630). One default at root + per-pillar overrides.

**Sitemap / robots (code, not files)**
- [ ] `app/sitemap.ts` returning the URL array (pull slugs from your data source, include `lastModified` from real `dateModified` — feeds the freshness signal). For a large hub, split into a sitemap index.
- [ ] `app/robots.ts` (no physical file) — allow indexing, reference the sitemap, and ⚠️ **explicitly do NOT block AI bots** (GPTBot/ClaudeBot/PerplexityBot/Google-Extended) since citations require crawl access.
- [ ] `JSON-LD` injected per-route (the Topic-1 graph) via a `<script type="application/ld+json">` in the server component.

**Structural**
- [ ] One `<h1>` per page; logical heading hierarchy (also AI-extraction + axe).
- [ ] `BreadcrumbList` schema + visible breadcrumbs on inner pages.
- [ ] Clean internal links (real `<a>`/`<Link>`, descriptive anchors — the silo from Topic 4).
- [ ] `lang` on `<html>`; `theme-color`; favicon set via `app/icon` convention.
- [ ] Validate every template through Google Rich Results Test + monitor Search Console Enhancements. ⚠️ Note the FAQ report retires June 2026 — don't gate on it.

**Best-practice combo per the docs:** server-rendered content + accurate per-route metadata + explicit canonicals + clean internal links + field-data performance checks = the full App-Router SEO stack. Metadata API as the single source of truth for titles/canonicals/cards; `generateMetadata` for dynamic routes; sitemap + robots via first-class routes.

## CROSS-CUTTING TAKEAWAYS (what to lock for the build)

1. **The site's real SEO/AI value is ENTITY TRUST, not rich snippets.** FAQ + HowTo rich results are DEAD (May 2026 / 2023). Build the `Organization` + `sameAs`(Wikidata-first) + `Person`(`knowsAbout`) graph — that's what feeds Knowledge Panel and AI citations. This is the single biggest 2026 shift to flag.
2. **AI-legibility = rendered HTML, not llms.txt.** Ship llms.txt as PROOF/signaling (it fits "the site is the proof" and the Agentic-OS story), but know it's ~0.1% of crawler traffic and Google ignores it. The real lever: server-rendered answer-first text, WORD-level (never per-character) heading animation, stats/quotes/citations, fresh dateModified, AI bots allowed in robots.
3. **INP is the CWV that fights your motion.** GSAP/Framer are heavy on the main thread; consider Motion.dev (8KB) or tightly scope/code-split, strip `will-change` after animations, push `"use client"` to leaves, use RSC + Suspense. Single variable font kills swap-CLS and the LCP-reflow trap.
4. **Next.js 16:** Turbopack default; PPR stable via `cacheComponents` (`"use cache"` for the static marketing shell); metadata/sitemap/robots all via first-class code routes; per-route canonical is the easiest audit win to not miss.
5. **The content hub IS the GEO engine** — pillar+cluster silo with named-expert E-E-A-T bylines ranks organically AND earns the AI citations, closing the loop with Topic 2. Quality-gate cluster depth (thin = cannibalization).
