Q3  —  Acid/base classification

Lewis acid/base is about electron pairs (accept/donate); Brønsted acid/base is specifically about protons (donate/accept H⁺). A species can be both, or Lewis-only.

B) Rank by strength (as a base)

NH₂⊖ — STRONG  ·  HO⊖ — STRONG  ·  CN⊖ — WEAK (conj. acid HCN pKa ≈ 9)  ·  cyclohexene — VERY WEAK

the weaker the conjugate acid holds its proton (lower pKa), the weaker the base.

C) Brønsted acid vs. base, defined

A Brønsted acid donates a proton; a Brønsted base accepts one. HCl + NH₃: HCl is the acid, NH₃ is the base.

Q5  —  Why carboxylic acid (pKa≈4) beats alcohol (pKa≈16)

Acidity is decided by the CONJUGATE BASE. Acetate spreads its −1 over two equivalent oxygens by resonance (each O ≈ −½) and the carbonyl withdraws inductively; ethoxide’s −1 sits on one oxygen with nowhere to go. A stabler anion = an easier-to-lose proton = 12 pKa units.

Acetic acid → acetate (resonance-stabilized)

Ethanol → ethoxide (localized — no resonance)

Q6  —  Products + classification

Reactions 1–3 share one 1° substrate — watch the reagent alone flip the outcome. Reactions 4–7 change substrate too (two alkenes, a benzylic bromide), where addition and substitution take over.

1) PhCH₂CH₂Br + KCN → PhCH₂CH₂C≡N

2) PhCH₂CH₂Br + KOH → PhCH₂CH₂OH

3) PhCH₂CH₂Br + KOtBu → PhCH=CH₂ (styrene)

4) 1-methylcyclopentene + HBr → 1-bromo-1-methylcyclopentane

5) PhCH₂Br + Li+ ⊖C≡C–CH₃ → PhCH₂C≡C–CH₃

6) Propene + HBr, peroxides/light → 1-bromopropane (anti-Markovnikov)

7) Propene + 1. BH₃ 2. NaOH/H₂O₂ → 1-propanol (anti-Markovnikov, syn addition)
