# question

Which CRM features matter most for running a small residential roofing company ($1.8M/yr, 15-19 jobs/mo, storm/insurance + retail replacement work, solo owner + crews, one office) — and which are worth adding to a lean custom CRM that already has a pipeline board, calendar, weekly attribution reports, and estimate logging?

## Answer

Bottom line: your CRM already has the operational spine (pipeline board, calendar, attribution, estimate logging). The four highest-leverage things to add next — in order — are **built-in two-way texting, job-attached photo documentation, insurance-claim/supplement status tracking, and automated follow-up on unsold estimates.** Everything else is a distant second, and some popular "enterprise" features (crew GPS tracking, heavy dispatch) are actively wrong for a shop your size.

Why texting is #1: poor communication is the single biggest homeowner complaint about roofers — roughly 40% name it, well ahead of workmanship (25%) or pricing (18%). It is the one thing most likely to cost you a bad review even when the roof is perfect. So the feature that moves the needle most is a **shared business SMS number inside the CRM** — one homeowner thread the office and you-in-the-truck both see, so history stops siloing in a personal phone. Add automated appointment/"on-the-way" reminders on top of it to cut no-shows.

For your storm/insurance half specifically: **timestamped photos auto-organized by job** and a **claim-status tracker** (claim #, adjuster, supplement stage) are what practitioners call the load-bearing insurance features — supplement negotiations run 2-8 weeks and both you and the homeowner need to see where it stands without a phone call.

For your retail-replacement half: **follow-up automation on unsold estimates** is the funnel-leakage plug that pairs directly with the estimate logging you already have.

What NOT to build: skip crew GPS/location tracking (privacy friction, real litigation exposure, and crews under ~5 coordinate by text anyway), and skip enterprise dispatch/per-seat modules — the recurring practitioner complaint about AccuLynx/ServiceTitan is paying enterprise money for features a small shop never touches. Lean is the correct instinct; add narrowly.

## Key findings

- **Two-way texting is the top add, not a nice-to-have.** Communication is the #1 homeowner complaint (~40%), and it is why the category leaders built texting natively. A shared/threaded business number (office + field on one conversation) is the specific implementation that matters. [verified]
- **Automated reminders convert directly to fewer no-shows.** Appointment + on-the-way texts are the concrete lever; homeowners broadly expect scheduling notifications by text. [verified — reminder mechanism; no-show % is vendor-cited, treat as directional]
- **Photo documentation is the insurance workhorse.** A detailed roofer names auto-chronological, per-job photo organization (CompanyCam-style) as his single most-loved feature for insurance work, and integration with measurement/accounting/photo/estimating tools (EagleView, QuickBooks, CompanyCam, Xactimate) as his #1 CRM criterion. [verified]
- **Insurance-claim/supplement tracking is a real, roofing-specific module** (claim #, adjuster, supplement stage) — the reason roofing CRMs exist versus generic field-service tools. [asserted, unverified — single vendor source]
- **Follow-up automation on unsold estimates** is the canonical retail-replacement funnel feature; it plugs the leak your attribution reports only measure. [asserted, unverified]
- **The "aerial measurement + photos are non-negotiable, tool fragmentation is the #1 complaint at $500-1,200/mo" narrative is not substantiated.** It traces to vendor SEO copy, not any real contractor study. The only defensible primary fact: aerial/satellite roof-measurement tools are used by **over half** of roofing contractors. Surface as a gap, not a finding. [verified as correction]
- **Don't over-build.** Independent review evidence says AccuLynx-class production/crew platforms are "overkill and overpriced" for 1-3 user shops; crew GPS tracking carries documented privacy/legal exposure and is coordinated manually by text at most small shops anyway. Your lean-CRM instinct is correct. [supported by corpus]
